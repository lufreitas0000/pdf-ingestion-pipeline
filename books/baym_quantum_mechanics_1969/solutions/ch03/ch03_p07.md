# Problem 3.7

**Source**: Chapter 3, Problem 7

## Problem Statement

7. Derive (3-89) from (3-88).

---

## Restatement

We are asked to derive the spatial form of the minimum uncertainty wave packet, equation (3-89), starting from the algebraic condition that the state satisfies the equality in the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, given by equation (3-88). 

Specifically, show that the condition:
$$
\left( \frac{\hat{x} - \langle x \rangle}{\Delta x} + i \frac{\hat{p}_x - \langle p_x \rangle}{\Delta p} \right) \ket{\Psi} = 0
$$
implies that the wave function in the position representation is:
$$
\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi} = \exp \left[ \frac{i x \langle p_x \rangle}{\hbar} - \frac{(x - \langle x \rangle)^2}{4(\Delta x)^2} \right] g(y, z)
$$
where $\Delta x \Delta p = \hbar / 2$ for a minimum uncertainty state, and $g(y, z)$ is an arbitrary function of the transverse coordinates. (Note: The text uses $\langle p \rangle$ to denote $\langle p_x \rangle$).

---

## Prerequisites

- **Position Basis Representation**: In the position basis, the position operator acts by multiplication and the momentum operator acts as a spatial derivative.
$$
\bra{\mathbf{r}} \hat{x} \ket{\Psi} = x \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi}, \quad \bra{\mathbf{r}} \hat{p}_x \ket{\Psi} = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi}
$$
- **Minimum Uncertainty Condition**: A state that satisfies the equality in the generalized uncertainty principle for $x$ and $p_x$ achieves the minimum possible uncertainty product:
$$
\Delta x \Delta p = \frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — Project the given operator equation (3-88) onto the position basis by applying the bra $\bra{\mathbf{r}}$.

> **Hint 2** — Substitute the position-space representations of the position and momentum operators, $\hat{x} \to x$ and $\hat{p}_x \to -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x}$.

> **Hint 3** — You will obtain a first-order separable differential equation for $\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi}$ with respect to $x$. Use the minimum uncertainty condition $\Delta x \Delta p = \hbar / 2$ to simplify the coefficients before integrating.

---

## Solution

### Setup

The condition for the minimum uncertainty wave packet, equation (3-88), is given by:
$$
\left( \frac{\hat{x} - \langle x \rangle}{\Delta x} + i \frac{\hat{p}_x - \langle p_x \rangle}{\Delta p} \right) \ket{\Psi} = 0
$$
We want to find the differential equation this implies for the wave function $\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi}$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 1: Project onto the position basis

We project the operator equation onto the position basis by acting on the left with $\bra{\mathbf{r}}$:
$$
\bra{\mathbf{r}} \left( \frac{\hat{x} - \langle x \rangle}{\Delta x} + i \frac{\hat{p}_x - \langle p_x \rangle}{\Delta p} \right) \ket{\Psi} = 0
$$
Using the position-space representations $\bra{\mathbf{r}} \hat{x} \ket{\Psi} = x \psi(\mathbf{r})$ and $\bra{\mathbf{r}} \hat{p}_x \ket{\Psi} = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \psi(\mathbf{r})$, we obtain:
$$
\frac{x - \langle x \rangle}{\Delta x} \psi(\mathbf{r}) + \frac{i}{\Delta p} \left( -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x} - \langle p_x \rangle \right) \psi(\mathbf{r}) = 0
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Simplify the differential equation

Distributing the terms and isolating the derivative of $\psi(\mathbf{r})$, we write:
$$
\frac{x - \langle x \rangle}{\Delta x} \psi(\mathbf{r}) + \frac{\hbar}{\Delta p} \frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r})}{\partial x} - i \frac{\langle p_x \rangle}{\Delta p} \psi(\mathbf{r}) = 0
$$
Rearranging to solve for the partial derivative with respect to $x$:
$$
\frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r})}{\partial x} = \left( -\frac{\Delta p}{\hbar \Delta x} (x - \langle x \rangle) + i \frac{\langle p_x \rangle}{\hbar} \right) \psi(\mathbf{r})
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Apply the minimum uncertainty condition

For a state that satisfies the equality in the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (a minimum uncertainty state), the product of the standard deviations is minimized:
$$
\Delta x \Delta p = \frac{\hbar}{2}
$$
We use this to eliminate $\Delta p$ from the coefficient of the $(x - \langle x \rangle)$ term:
$$
\frac{\Delta p}{\hbar \Delta x} = \frac{\hbar / (2 \Delta x)}{\hbar \Delta x} = \frac{1}{2 (\Delta x)^2}
$$
Substituting this back into the differential equation yields:
$$
\frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r})}{\partial x} = \left( -\frac{x - \langle x \rangle}{2 (\Delta x)^2} + i \frac{\langle p_x \rangle}{\hbar} \right) \psi(\mathbf{r})
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Integrate the differential equation

This is a separable first-order differential equation for the $x$-dependence of $\psi(\mathbf{r})$. We integrate with respect to $x$:
$$
\int \frac{1}{\psi(\mathbf{r})} \frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r})}{\partial x} \, \dd x = \int \left( -\frac{x - \langle x \rangle}{2 (\Delta x)^2} + i \frac{\langle p_x \rangle}{\hbar} \right) \dd x
$$
Evaluating the integrals:
$$
\ln \psi(\mathbf{r}) = -\frac{(x - \langle x \rangle)^2}{4 (\Delta x)^2} + i \frac{\langle p_x \rangle x}{\hbar} + f(y, z)
$$
where $f(y, z)$ is an arbitrary function of the integration variables $y$ and $z$ (the "constant" of integration with respect to $x$). 

Exponentiating both sides, we find the general solution:
$$
\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \exp \left[ \frac{i \langle p_x \rangle x}{\hbar} - \frac{(x - \langle x \rangle)^2}{4(\Delta x)^2} \right] g(y, z)
$$
where $g(y, z) = e^{f(y, z)}$. Replacing $\langle p_x \rangle$ with $\langle p \rangle$ to match the book's notation gives exactly the desired result.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\Psi} = \exp \left[ \frac{i x \langle p \rangle}{\hbar} - \frac{(x - \langle x \rangle)^2}{4(\Delta x)^2} \right] g(y, z)}
$$

---

## Discussion

This result demonstrates that the minimum uncertainty wave packet (which saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty principle) must be a Gaussian function in position space. The real part of the exponent provides the localization in space (a normal distribution centered at $\langle x \rangle$ with standard deviation $\Delta x$), while the imaginary part gives the state an average momentum $\langle p \rangle$. Note that the Fourier transform of a Gaussian is also a Gaussian, so the momentum-space wave function $\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\Psi}$ will correspondingly have a Gaussian profile.
