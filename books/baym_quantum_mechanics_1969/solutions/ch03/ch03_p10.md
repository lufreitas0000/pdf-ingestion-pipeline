# Problem 3.10

**Source**: Chapter 3, Problem 10

## Problem Statement

10. (a) The probability current density $\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ is given in terms of the wave function by
    $$
    \mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t) = \frac{1}{2m} \left[ \psi^*(\mathbf{r}, t) \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) - \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^*(\mathbf{r}, t) \cdot \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) \right].
    $$
    Show directly from Schrödinger's equation that the probability density, $P(\mathbf{r}, t) = |\psi(\mathbf{r}, t)|^2$ and the probability current density, obey the continuity equation
    $$
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t} P(\mathbf{r}, t) + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t) = 0.
    $$
    Do not assume that the potential vanishes.
    (b) What is the form for $\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ when there is a magnetic field present specified by the vector potential $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}, t)$?

---

## Restatement

Given the probability current density $\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t)$, use the time-dependent Schrödinger equation to prove the continuity equation $\frac{\partial}{\partial t} P(\mathbf{r}, t) + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j}(\mathbf{r}, t) = 0$, where $P(\mathbf{r}, t) = \psi^*(\mathbf{r}, t)\psi(\mathbf{r}, t)$ is the probability density. Then, determine the modified form of the probability current density $\mathbf{j}_A(\mathbf{r}, t)$ when a magnetic field is present, represented by a vector potential $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}, t)$.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Schrödinger Equation**: The time evolution of a quantum state is given by $i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \hat{H}\psi$.
- **Probability Density**: $P(\mathbf{r}, t) = |\psi(\mathbf{r}, t)|^2 = \psi^*(\mathbf{r}, t)\psi(\mathbf{r}, t)$.
- **Hamiltonian with Vector Potential**: The Hamiltonian for a charged particle in a magnetic field is $\hat{H} = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \hat{\mathbf{p}} - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \right)^2 + V(\mathbf{r}, t)$.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For part (a), start by taking the partial time derivative of $P(\mathbf{r}, t) = \psi^*\psi$ using the product rule.
> **Hint 2** — Substitute $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}$ and $\frac{\partial \psi^*}{\partial t}$ using the Schrödinger equation and its complex conjugate.
> **Hint 3** — Group the terms to form a divergence, showing that the potential $V$ cancels out because it is real.
> **Hint 4** — For part (b), replace the momentum operator $\hat{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla$ with the kinematic momentum operator $\hat{\mathbf{\Pi}} = \hat{\mathbf{p}} - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A}$ in the definition of the current density.

---

## Solution

### Step 1: Time Derivative of Probability Density

We start with the probability density $P(\mathbf{r}, t) = \psi^*\psi$. The partial time derivative is:
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial \psi^*}{\partial t}\psi + \psi^*\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Substitute the Schrödinger Equation

The time-dependent Schrödinger equation and its complex conjugate are:
$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi \implies \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{i\hbar} \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi \right)
$$
$$
-i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi^*}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi^* + V\psi^* \implies \frac{\partial \psi^*}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{i\hbar} \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi^* + V\psi^* \right)
$$
where we assumed the potential $V(\mathbf{r}, t)$ is real ($V = V^*$).
Substituting these into the time derivative of $P$:
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{i\hbar} \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi^* + V\psi^* \right)\psi + \psi^*\frac{1}{i\hbar} \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi \right)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Simplify and Rearrange

Expanding the terms, the potential $V$ cancels out:
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2mi\hbar}(\psi\nabla^2\psi^* - \psi^*\nabla^2\psi) - \frac{V\psi^*\psi}{i\hbar} + \frac{V\psi^*\psi}{i\hbar}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} (\psi\nabla^2\psi^* - \psi^*\nabla^2\psi)
$$
We can rewrite the right-hand side as a divergence:
$$
\nabla \cdot (\psi^*\nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\psi^*) = \nabla\psi^*\cdot\nabla\psi + \psi^*\nabla^2\psi - \nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\psi^* - \psi\nabla^2\psi^* = \psi^*\nabla^2\psi - \psi\nabla^2\psi^*
$$
Thus, we have:
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar}{2mi} \nabla \cdot (\psi^*\nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\psi^*)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Continuity Equation

We recognize the term inside the divergence as related to the given current density $\mathbf{j}$:
$$
\mathbf{j} = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \psi^* \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi - \psi \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^* \right) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} (\psi^*\nabla\psi - \psi\nabla\psi^*)
$$
Substituting this into our equation for $\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}$:
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{j} \implies \frac{\partial P}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j} = 0
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Probability Current with a Magnetic Field

In the presence of a magnetic field specified by a vector potential $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}, t)$, the momentum operator $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ is replaced by the kinematic momentum operator $\hat{\mathbf{\Pi}} = \hat{\mathbf{p}} - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} = \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A}$. The Hamiltonian is:
$$
\hat{H} = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \hat{\mathbf{p}} - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \right)^2 + V = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \right)^2 + V
$$
To find the new probability current density $\mathbf{j}_A$, we can require that the continuity equation still holds, $\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j}_A = 0$, but using the new Hamiltonian to evaluate $i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \hat{H}\psi$.
Alternatively, we generalize the definition of $\mathbf{j}$ by replacing the momentum operators with kinematic momentum operators, ensuring that $\mathbf{j}_A$ is real and gauge-invariant:
$$
\mathbf{j}_A = \frac{1}{2m} \left[ \psi^* \hat{\mathbf{\Pi}} \psi + \psi (\hat{\mathbf{\Pi}}\psi)^* \right] = \frac{1}{2m} \left[ \psi^* \left( \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \right) \psi + \psi \left( \left( \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \right) \psi \right)^* \right]
$$
Evaluating the complex conjugate term:
$$
\left( \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A}\psi \right)^* = -\frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^* - \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A}\psi^*
$$
Substituting this back into the current:
$$
\mathbf{j}_A = \frac{1}{2m} \left[ \psi^* \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi - \psi^* \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \psi - \psi \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^* - \psi \frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A} \psi^* \right]
$$
Grouping the gradient terms and the vector potential terms:
$$
\mathbf{j}_A = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \psi^* \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi - \psi \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^* \right) - \frac{e}{mc}\mathbf{A}|\psi|^2
$$
$$
\mathbf{j}_A = \mathbf{j} - \frac{e}{mc}\mathbf{A} P(\mathbf{r}, t)
$$
where $\mathbf{j}$ is the free-particle probability current density.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j} = 0}
$$
$$
\boxed{\mathbf{j}_A(\mathbf{r}, t) = \frac{1}{2m} \left[ \psi^* \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi - \psi \frac{\hbar}{i}\nabla \psi^* \right] - \frac{e}{mc}\mathbf{A}|\psi|^2}
$$

---

## Discussion

The continuity equation $\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{j} = 0$ expresses the local conservation of probability: any change in the probability of finding a particle in a given region must be accompanied by a flow of probability current into or out of that region. The assumption that the potential $V$ is real is crucial; an imaginary component in the potential would act as a sink or source of probability, breaking the local conservation.

When a magnetic field is introduced via the vector potential $\mathbf{A}$, the probability current density acquires an additional term proportional to $\mathbf{A}|\psi|^2$. This modified current $\mathbf{j}_A$ is gauge invariant, which is a necessary physical requirement since probability and its flow must be independent of the choice of gauge. In contrast, the wave function $\psi$ and the free current $\mathbf{j}$ are gauge dependent.
