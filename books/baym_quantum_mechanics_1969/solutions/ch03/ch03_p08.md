# Problem 3.8

**Source**: Chapter 3, Problem 8

## Problem Statement

8. Verify (3-98) by explicit integration using (3-93).

---

## Restatement

Verify the composition property of the quantum mechanical propagator (equation 3-98),
$$
\int \dd^3 r_2\, K(\mathbf{r}_1 t_1, \mathbf{r}_2 t_2) K(\mathbf{r}_2 t_2, \mathbf{r}_3 t_3) = K(\mathbf{r}_1 t_1, \mathbf{r}_3 t_3)
$$
by explicit integration using the free particle propagator (equation 3-93),
$$
K(\mathbf{r} t, \mathbf{r}' t') = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar (t - t')} \right)^{3/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')^2}{2\hbar(t - t')} \right]
$$

---

## Prerequisites

- **Free Particle Propagator**: The transition amplitude for a free particle to travel from $(\mathbf{r}', t')$ to $(\mathbf{r}, t)$.
  $$ K(\mathbf{r} t, \mathbf{r}' t') = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar (t - t')} \right)^{3/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')^2}{2\hbar(t - t')} \right] $$
- **Propagator Composition**: The total amplitude to go from a starting point to an endpoint is the integral over all intermediate points at an intermediate time of the product of the intermediate amplitudes. This is the foundation of the path integral formulation.
- **Gaussian Integral**: The standard identity for evaluating integrals of complex exponentials with quadratic arguments.
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-a x^2} \, \dd x = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}} $$

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — Write out the full 3D integral using the explicit form of the free particle propagator. Note that the 3D integral separates completely into three independent, identical 1D integrals over $x_2$, $y_2$, and $z_2$.

> **Hint 2** — In the 1D integral over $x_2$, combine the exponentials and group the terms proportional to $x_2^2$, $x_2$, and the terms independent of $x_2$. Then, complete the square for the variable $x_2$.

> **Hint 3** — Perform the Gaussian integral over $x_2$. Remember the identity $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-a x^2} \, \dd x = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}$.

> **Hint 4** — Simplify the remaining terms in the exponent and the normalization prefactors. The combined prefactors from the two propagators and the result of the Gaussian integral should yield the exact prefactor for a free particle propagating over the total time $t_1 - t_3$.

---

## Solution

### Setup

Let $\tau_1 = t_1 - t_2$ and $\tau_2 = t_2 - t_3$. Note that $\tau_1 + \tau_2 = t_1 - t_3$.
We wish to compute the integral:
$$
I = \int \dd^3 r_2\, \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \tau_1} \right)^{3/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2)^2}{2\hbar \tau_1} \right] \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \tau_2} \right)^{3/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(\mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_3)^2}{2\hbar \tau_2} \right]
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 1: Separating into 1D Integrals

The 3D integral completely factorizes into three identical 1D integrals for the $x$, $y$, and $z$ components. We can write $I = I_x I_y I_z$, where:
$$
I_x = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \tau_1} \right)^{1/2} \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \tau_2} \right)^{1/2} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \dd x_2 \, \exp \left[ \frac{im}{2\hbar} \left( \frac{(x_1 - x_2)^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{(x_2 - x_3)^2}{\tau_2} \right) \right]
$$
We will evaluate $I_x$ and then cube the result to find $I$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Completing the Square in the Exponent

Let us focus on the quantity in the parentheses in the exponent:
$$
E_x = \frac{(x_2 - x_1)^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{(x_2 - x_3)^2}{\tau_2}
$$
Expand and collect terms with respect to $x_2$:
$$
E_x = x_2^2 \left( \frac{1}{\tau_1} + \frac{1}{\tau_2} \right) - 2x_2 \left( \frac{x_1}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3}{\tau_2} \right) + \left( \frac{x_1^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3^2}{\tau_2} \right)
$$
Let $A = \frac{1}{\tau_1} + \frac{1}{\tau_2} = \frac{\tau_1 + \tau_2}{\tau_1 \tau_2}$ and $B = \frac{x_1}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3}{\tau_2}$. 
Completing the square yields:
$$
E_x = A \left( x_2 - \frac{B}{A} \right)^2 + C - \frac{B^2}{A}
$$
where $C = \frac{x_1^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3^2}{\tau_2}$. The constant part is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C - \frac{B^2}{A} &= \left( \frac{x_1^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3^2}{\tau_2} \right) - \frac{\tau_1 \tau_2}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} \left( \frac{x_1}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3}{\tau_2} \right)^2 \\
&= \frac{1}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} \left[ (\tau_1 + \tau_2) \left( \frac{x_1^2}{\tau_1} + \frac{x_3^2}{\tau_2} \right) - \tau_1 \tau_2 \left( \frac{x_1^2}{\tau_1^2} + \frac{x_3^2}{\tau_2^2} + \frac{2x_1 x_3}{\tau_1 \tau_2} \right) \right] \\
&= \frac{1}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} \left[ x_1^2 + \frac{\tau_2}{\tau_1} x_1^2 + \frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2} x_3^2 + x_3^2 - \frac{\tau_2}{\tau_1} x_1^2 - \frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2} x_3^2 - 2x_1 x_3 \right] \\
&= \frac{1}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} (x_1^2 + x_3^2 - 2x_1 x_3) = \frac{(x_1 - x_3)^2}{\tau_1 + \tau_2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, the exponent factorizes nicely into a term dependent on $x_2$ and a term independent of $x_2$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Performing the Gaussian Integral

Substitute the completed square back into the integral $I_x$:
$$
I_x = \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \sqrt{\tau_1 \tau_2}} \exp \left[ \frac{im(x_1 - x_3)^2}{2\hbar(\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right] \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \dd x_2 \, \exp \left[ \frac{im}{2\hbar} A \left( x_2 - \frac{B}{A} \right)^2 \right]
$$
Using the standard Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-a u^2} \, \dd u = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}$ with $a = -\frac{im A}{2\hbar}$ (we implicitly assume a small positive imaginary part to the mass or time to ensure convergence):
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \dd x_2 \, \exp \left[ \frac{im}{2\hbar} A \left( x_2 - \frac{B}{A} \right)^2 \right] = \sqrt{ \frac{2\pi i \hbar}{m A} } = \sqrt{ \frac{2\pi i \hbar}{m} \frac{\tau_1 \tau_2}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} }
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Simplifying the Prefactor and Exponent

Multiply the constant prefactor by the result of the Gaussian integral:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Prefactor of } I_x &= \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar \sqrt{\tau_1 \tau_2}} \sqrt{ \frac{2\pi i \hbar}{m} \frac{\tau_1 \tau_2}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} } \\
&= \sqrt{ \frac{m}{2\pi i \hbar} \frac{1}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} } = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i \hbar (\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right)^{1/2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, for the 1D integral $I_x$, we have:
$$
I_x = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i \hbar (\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right)^{1/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(x_1 - x_3)^2}{2\hbar(\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right]
$$
This is precisely the 1D free particle propagator from $t_3$ to $t_1$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

Since the 3D integral $I$ is the product of three identical 1D integrals $I_x I_y I_z$, we simply cube the prefactor and sum the exponents for the three coordinates:
$$
I = \left( \frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar (\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right)^{3/2} \exp \left[ \frac{im(\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_3)^2}{2\hbar(\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \right]
$$
Recalling that $\tau_1 + \tau_2 = t_1 - t_3$, we finally obtain:
$$
\boxed{ \int \dd^3 r_2\, K(\mathbf{r}_1 t_1, \mathbf{r}_2 t_2) K(\mathbf{r}_2 t_2, \mathbf{r}_3 t_3) = K(\mathbf{r}_1 t_1, \mathbf{r}_3 t_3) }
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

## Discussion

This calculation verifies the composition property (also known as the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation or semi-group property) for the free particle propagator. Physically, it embodies the principle of superposition: the total quantum mechanical amplitude for a particle to travel from an initial point $(\mathbf{r}_3, t_3)$ to a final point $(\mathbf{r}_1, t_1)$ is the sum (integral) over all possible intermediate positions $\mathbf{r}_2$ at time $t_2$ of the amplitude to go via $\mathbf{r}_2$. This property is the foundational postulate from which Feynman's path integral formulation of quantum mechanics is derived, allowing continuous time-slicing to construct amplitudes for arbitrary potentials.
