# Problem 3.2

**Source**: Chapter 3, Problem 2

## Problem Statement

2. (a) Write out $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ as the product of three one-dimensional delta functions in spherical coordinates.
   (b) What is the Fourier transform of $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$? Write out $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ in terms of its Fourier transform.
   (c) Show that $\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = (2\pi\hbar)^3 \delta(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}')$.

---

## Restatement

We are given the 3D Dirac delta function $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ and the position and momentum representations of quantum states. 
(a) Express $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ as a product of three one-dimensional delta functions in spherical coordinates $(r, \theta, \phi)$.
(b) Find the spatial Fourier transform of $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ with respect to momentum $\mathbf{p}$, and express $\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$ as the inverse Fourier transform.
(c) Given the position representation of a momentum eigenstate $\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{p}} = e^{i\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{r}/\hbar}$, prove the orthogonality relation $\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = (2\pi\hbar)^3 \delta(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}')$.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Properties of the Delta Function**: The 3D delta function is defined by its integral with an arbitrary test function: $\int \dd^3 r \, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') f(\mathbf{r}) = f(\mathbf{r}')$.
- **Volume Element in Spherical Coordinates**: In spherical coordinates, the differential volume element is $\dd^3 r = r^2 \sin\theta \, \dd r \, \dd\theta \, \dd\phi$.
- **Fourier Transform and its Inverse**: The spatial Fourier transform of a function $f(\mathbf{r})$ into momentum space is $\tilde{f}(\mathbf{p}) = \int \dd^3 r \, e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar} f(\mathbf{r})$, with the inverse transformation being $f(\mathbf{r}) = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar} \tilde{f}(\mathbf{p})$.
- **Completeness Relation**: The position basis states satisfy the completeness relation $\int \dd^3 r \, \ket{\mathbf{r}}\bra{\mathbf{r}} = \hat{1}$.
- **Scaling of the Delta Function**: In $n$ dimensions, $\delta(a\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{|a|^n} \delta(\mathbf{x})$.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For part (a), the integral of the 3D delta function over all space must equal 1. Use the volume element in spherical coordinates and match it with the product of 1D delta functions $\delta(r-r')\delta(\theta-\theta')\delta(\phi-\phi')$.

> **Hint 2** — For part (b), apply the standard definition of the spatial Fourier transform to the function $f(\mathbf{r}) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$. Then substitute the result into the inverse Fourier transform to get an integral representation of the delta function.

> **Hint 3** — For part (c), insert the completeness relation for the position basis $\int \dd^3 r \, \ket{\mathbf{r}}\bra{\mathbf{r}} = \hat{1}$ between the momentum states $\bra{\mathbf{p}}$ and $\ket{\mathbf{p}'}$.

> **Hint 4** — Use the integral identity derived in part (b) (with the roles of position and momentum coordinates swapped) to evaluate the spatial integral in part (c).

---

## Solution

### Setup

The 3D Dirac delta function in Cartesian coordinates is defined such that its volume integral is unity: $\int \dd^3 r \, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = 1$.

### Step 1: Delta Function in Spherical Coordinates

The volume element in spherical coordinates is $\dd^3 r = r^2 \sin\theta \, \dd r \, \dd\theta \, \dd\phi$.
Integrating the delta function over all space gives:
$$
\int \dd^3 r \, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = \int_{0}^{\infty} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} r^2 \sin\theta \, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') \, \dd r \, \dd\theta \, \dd\phi = 1
$$
For the integrand to evaluate to 1 and act as a delta function at the point $(r', \theta', \phi')$, we require a product of 1D delta functions divided by the Jacobian factor:
$$
\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = \frac{1}{r^2 \sin\theta} \delta(r - r') \delta(\theta - \theta') \delta(\phi - \phi')
$$
This ensures that upon integration, the $r^2 \sin\theta$ factor from the volume element is exactly cancelled out.

### Step 2: Fourier Transform of the Delta Function

The spatial Fourier transform of a function $f(\mathbf{r})$ with respect to momentum $\mathbf{p}$ is given by:
$$
\tilde{f}(\mathbf{p}) = \int \dd^3 r \, e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar} f(\mathbf{r})
$$
Substituting $f(\mathbf{r}) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$, and evaluating the integral over the delta function, we get:
$$
\tilde{f}(\mathbf{p}) = \int \dd^3 r \, e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar} \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}'/\hbar}
$$
The inverse Fourier transform is defined as:
$$
f(\mathbf{r}) = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar} \tilde{f}(\mathbf{p})
$$
Substituting our transform $\tilde{f}(\mathbf{p})$ back in gives the integral representation of the delta function:
$$
\delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')/\hbar}
$$

### Step 3: Orthogonality of Momentum States

We wish to evaluate the inner product $\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'}$. We can insert the completeness relation for the position basis, $\int \dd^3 r \, \ket{\mathbf{r}}\bra{\mathbf{r}} = \hat{1}$:
$$
\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = \int \dd^3 r \, \braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{r}} \braket{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{p}'}
$$
Using the position representation of the momentum eigenstates, $\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{p}'} = e^{i\mathbf{p}'\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar}$ and its complex conjugate $\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{r}} = e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar}$, the integral becomes:
$$
\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = \int \dd^3 r \, e^{i(\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p})\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar}
$$
From the integral representation of the delta function in Step 2, we know that:
$$
\int \dd^3 x \, e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}} = (2\pi)^3 \delta(\mathbf{k})
$$
Letting $\mathbf{k} = (\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p})/\hbar$ and $\dd^3 x = \dd^3 r$, we obtain:
$$
\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = (2\pi)^3 \delta\left(\frac{\mathbf{p}' - \mathbf{p}}{\hbar}\right)
$$
Using the 3-dimensional scaling property of the delta function, $\delta(\mathbf{a}/\hbar) = \hbar^3 \delta(\mathbf{a})$, we get:
$$
\braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = (2\pi\hbar)^3 \delta(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}')
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{(a)} \quad & \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = \frac{1}{r^2 \sin\theta} \delta(r - r') \delta(\theta - \theta') \delta(\phi - \phi') \\
\text{(b)} \quad & \text{F.T.} = e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{r}'/\hbar}, \quad \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')/\hbar} \\
\text{(c)} \quad & \braket{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{p}'} = (2\pi\hbar)^3 \delta(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}')
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Discussion

The representation of the delta function in spherical coordinates requires the inverse of the Jacobian determinant to ensure that the volume integral yields unity. The non-uniformity of the volume element near the origin ($r \to 0$) or the poles ($\theta \to 0, \pi$) causes the coordinate expression to become singular, which demands careful treatment when evaluating integrals at these points.
The momentum space orthogonality relation differs from the standard Kronecker delta because momentum is a continuous variable. The normalization factor $(2\pi\hbar)^3$ directly arises from the choice of normalization for the plane wave position states, $\braket{\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{p}} = e^{i\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{r}/\hbar}$.
