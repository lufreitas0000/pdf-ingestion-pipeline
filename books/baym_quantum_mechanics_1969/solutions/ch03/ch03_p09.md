# Problem 3.9

**Source**: Chapter 3, Problem 9

## Problem Statement

9. (a) Show that the free particle propagator $K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t')$ obeys the free particle Schrödinger equation
   $$
   \left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} + \frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m} \right) K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = 0.
   $$
   (b) Suppose that a particle is acted on by a potential $v(\mathbf{r}, t)$, and that its wave function at $t_0$ is $\psi_0(\mathbf{r})$. Show that the wave function of the particle at a later time $t$ is given as the solution to the integral equation
   $$
   \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) = \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t_0) \psi_0(\mathbf{r}') + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t').
   $$

---

## Restatement

Show that the free particle propagator $K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t')$ satisfies the free particle Schrödinger equation. Then, consider a particle in a potential $v(\mathbf{r}, t)$ with initial state $\psi(\mathbf{r}, t_0) = \psi_0(\mathbf{r})$. Show that the time evolution of the wave function is equivalently described by an integral equation involving the free particle propagator and a source term from the potential.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Free Particle Propagator**: The amplitude for a free particle to propagate from $(\mathbf{r}', t')$ to $(\mathbf{r}, t)$, given in momentum space representation by
  $$ K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')/\hbar} e^{-i\frac{p^2}{2m}(t-t')/\hbar}. $$
- **Initial Condition**: At equal times, the propagator is a delta function: $K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$.
- **Leibniz Integral Rule**: The derivative of a definite integral with variable limits includes the integrand evaluated at the limits plus the integral of the derivative:
  $$ \frac{d}{dt} \int_{a}^{t} f(t, t') \, \dd t' = f(t, t) + \int_{a}^{t} \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} \, \dd t' $$

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For part (a), act with the Schrödinger operator directly on the momentum-space integral representation of the propagator $K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t')$.

> **Hint 2** — For part (b), verify the initial condition at $t = t_0$, then apply the full Schrödinger operator $(i\hbar \partial_t - \hat{H}_0)$ to both sides of the given integral equation.

> **Hint 3** — When differentiating the time integral with respect to $t$, remember to use the Leibniz rule to differentiate the upper limit of integration.

> **Hint 4** — Evaluate the boundary term that appears from the upper limit by utilizing the fact that $\lim_{t' \to t} K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$.

---

## Solution

### Step 1: The free particle propagator equation

We write out the explicit momentum space representation of the free particle propagator:
$$
K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \exp\left( \frac{i}{\hbar} \mathbf{p} \cdot (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') - \frac{i}{\hbar} \frac{p^2}{2m} (t - t') \right).
$$
Applying the time derivative operator inside the integral:
$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \left( \frac{p^2}{2m} \right) \exp\left( \frac{i}{\hbar} \mathbf{p} \cdot (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') - \frac{i}{\hbar} \frac{p^2}{2m} (t - t') \right).
$$
Next, we apply the Laplacian operator (multiplied by $\hbar^2/2m$):
$$
\frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m} K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left( \frac{i\mathbf{p}}{\hbar} \right)^2 \exp\left( \dots \right) = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \left( -\frac{p^2}{2m} \right) \exp\left( \dots \right).
$$
Adding the two results yields:
$$
\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} + \frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m} \right) K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \left( \frac{p^2}{2m} - \frac{p^2}{2m} \right) \exp\left( \dots \right) = 0.
$$
This completes part (a).
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Formulating the full Schrödinger equation

For part (b), we define the free particle Hamiltonian operator as $\hat{H}_0 = -\frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m}$. The total Hamiltonian is $\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 + v(\mathbf{r}, t)$. The full Schrödinger equation we wish to recover is:
$$
\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) = v(\mathbf{r}, t) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t).
$$
We must show that the given integral equation:
$$
\psi(\mathbf{r}, t) = \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t_0) \psi_0(\mathbf{r}') + \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t')
$$
satisfies this differential equation with the correct initial condition.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Verifying the initial condition

Taking the limit as $t \to t_0$, the second term (the integral over $t'$) vanishes because the integration limits become identical. The first term evaluates to:
$$
\psi(\mathbf{r}, t_0) = \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t_0, \mathbf{r}'t_0) \psi_0(\mathbf{r}').
$$
At equal times, the propagator becomes a spatial delta function:
$$
K(\mathbf{r}t_0, \mathbf{r}'t_0) = \int \frac{\dd^3 p}{(2\pi\hbar)^3} \exp\left( \frac{i}{\hbar} \mathbf{p} \cdot (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') \right) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}').
$$
Thus:
$$
\psi(\mathbf{r}, t_0) = \int \dd^3 r'\, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') \psi_0(\mathbf{r}') = \psi_0(\mathbf{r}).
$$
This verifies that the specified initial condition is satisfied.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Applying the Schrödinger operator to the integral equation

We apply the free-particle Schrödinger operator $\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right)$ to the right-hand side of the integral equation.

For the first term (the homogeneous solution):
$$
\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right) \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t_0) \psi_0(\mathbf{r}') = \int \dd^3 r'\, \left[ \left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right) K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t_0) \right] \psi_0(\mathbf{r}') = 0,
$$
where we used the result from part (a).

For the second term (the driven solution), we apply the time derivative using the Leibniz integral rule:
$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left[ \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t') \right] = \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t) v(\mathbf{r}', t) \psi(\mathbf{r}', t) + \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, \left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') \right) v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t').
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Evaluating the driven terms

In the first piece, we again substitute $K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t) = \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}')$:
$$
\int \dd^3 r'\, \delta(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}') v(\mathbf{r}', t) \psi(\mathbf{r}', t) = v(\mathbf{r}, t) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t).
$$

Applying the spatial operator $-\hat{H}_0$ simply passes through the integration over $t'$ and $\mathbf{r}'$:
$$
-\hat{H}_0 \left[ \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t') \right] = \frac{1}{i\hbar} \int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, \left( -\hat{H}_0 K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t') \right) v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t').
$$

Combining the time derivative and spatial operator under the integral:
$$
\int_{t_0}^t \dd t' \int \dd^3 r'\, \underbrace{\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right) K(\mathbf{r}t, \mathbf{r}'t')}_{= 0 \text{ from part (a)}} \frac{1}{i\hbar} v(\mathbf{r}', t') \psi(\mathbf{r}', t') = 0.
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

Putting it all together, we obtain:
$$
\left( i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} - \hat{H}_0 \right) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) = 0 + v(\mathbf{r}, t) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) + 0
$$
$$
\boxed{ i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi(\mathbf{r}, t)}{\partial t} = \left( -\frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m} + v(\mathbf{r}, t) \right) \psi(\mathbf{r}, t) }
$$
Since the integral equation satisfies both the initial condition and the full differential Schrödinger equation, it specifies the exact time evolution of the wave function.

---

## Discussion

The integral equation derived is fundamentally a form of the Lippmann-Schwinger equation translated into the time domain. It intuitively expresses the full wave function as the sum of a free-particle state (the initial wave packet evolving as if no potential were present) and a "scattered wave." The second term is interpreted physically using Huygens' principle: it represents the sum over all intermediate times $t'$ and positions $\mathbf{r}'$ of the particle arriving at $\mathbf{r}'$, interacting with the potential $v$ (which acts as a source of new spherical waves), and then propagating freely from $\mathbf{r}'$ to its final position $\mathbf{r}$. This integral form is highly useful in time-dependent perturbation theory (leading directly to the Born series), where one iteratively plugs the zero-order free-particle solution into the right-hand side to approximate the interacting wave function.
