# Problem 2.2

**Source**: Chapter 2, Problem 2

## Problem Statement

2. Consider a 30° polarized photon (as in Problem 9, Chapter 1) that passes through a right circular prism and then a left circular prism. What is the amplitude for the photon to pass through both prisms?

---

## Restatement

Consider a photon initially prepared in a linearly polarized state at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the $x$-axis, given by $\ket{30^\circ} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\ket{x} + \frac{1}{2}\ket{y}$. The photon is sent through a sequence of two polarizing prisms. The first prism transmits only right circularly polarized light, corresponding to the state $\ket{R} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} + i\ket{y})$. The second prism transmits only left circularly polarized light, corresponding to the state $\ket{L} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} - i\ket{y})$. Calculate the probability amplitude for the initially $30^\circ$-polarized photon to pass through both prisms.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Circular Polarization States**: The right and left circularly polarized states in the linear basis are $\ket{R} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} + i\ket{y})$ and $\ket{L} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} - i\ket{y})$.
- **Measurement and Projection**: A prism that transmits only a specific state $\ket{\psi}$ acts mathematically as the projection operator $\hat{P}_\psi = \ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}$ on the incident state vector.
- **Sequential Measurements**: The probability amplitude for an initial state $\ket{\psi_i}$ to pass through a sequence of projections (first onto $\ket{\phi_1}$, then onto $\ket{\phi_2}$) is obtained by applying the projection operators in sequence: $\bra{\phi_2} (\ket{\phi_2}\bra{\phi_2}) (\ket{\phi_1}\bra{\phi_1}) \ket{\psi_i} = \braket{\phi_2}{\phi_1}\braket{\phi_1}{\psi_i}$.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — Represent the transmission through the two prisms as a sequence of projection operators acting on the initial photon state.
> **Hint 2** — The overall amplitude involves the term $\braket{L}{R}$. Evaluate this inner product before calculating the full expression.
> **Hint 3** — Remember that $\bra{L}$ is the Hermitian conjugate of $\ket{L}$. Be careful with the sign of the imaginary unit $i$ when taking the bra.

---

## Solution

### Step 1: Formulate the Amplitude Expression

The amplitude for the initial state $\ket{30^\circ}$ to pass through the right circular prism ($\ket{R}$) and then the left circular prism ($\ket{L}$) is given by applying the corresponding sequence of projection operators and then taking the inner product with the final measured state $\ket{L}$.

$$
\text{Amplitude} = \bra{L} \left( \ket{L}\bra{L} \right) \left( \ket{R}\bra{R} \right) \ket{30^\circ}
$$

Since the states are normalized, $\braket{L}{L} = 1$, the expression simplifies to the product of two inner products.

$$
\text{Amplitude} = \braket{L}{R} \braket{R}{30^\circ}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Evaluate the Inner Product Between Prisms

We must determine the transition amplitude $\braket{L}{R}$ between the right and left circularly polarized states. First, we write the bra vector $\bra{L}$ by taking the Hermitian conjugate of $\ket{L} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} - i\ket{y})$.

$$
\bra{L} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\bra{x} + i\bra{y})
$$

Now we compute the inner product with $\ket{R} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} + i\ket{y})$.

$$
\braket{L}{R} = \left[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\bra{x} + i\bra{y}) \right] \left[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{x} + i\ket{y}) \right]
$$

Expanding this product and using the orthonormality of the linear basis ($\braket{x}{x} = \braket{y}{y} = 1$ and $\braket{x}{y} = \braket{y}{x} = 0$):

$$
\braket{L}{R} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \braket{x}{x} + i\braket{x}{y} + i\braket{y}{x} + i^2\braket{y}{y} \right) = \frac{1}{2} (1 + 0 + 0 - 1) = 0
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Determine the Final Amplitude

Substitute the result $\braket{L}{R} = 0$ back into our total amplitude expression from Step 1.

$$
\text{Amplitude} = 0 \times \braket{R}{30^\circ} = 0
$$

The calculation of $\braket{R}{30^\circ}$ is unnecessary because the intermediate states are completely orthogonal.

### Result

$$
\boxed{\text{Amplitude} = 0}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

---

## Discussion

Right and left circular polarizations form a mutually orthogonal basis for photon polarization states. Analogous to crossed linear polarizers (e.g., $x$ and $y$), sequential measurements of mutually orthogonal states yield zero probability of transmission. The photon, once projected into the right circularly polarized state by the first prism, has absolutely zero probability of possessing left circular polarization, making the initial polarization angle of $30^\circ$ physically irrelevant to the final outcome.
