# Problem 1.4: Adjoint Operators and Eigenvalues

## Restatement
Consider an arbitrary linear operator $M$ acting on a complex Hilbert space, and let $\ket{\Phi}$ and $\ket{\Psi}$ be any two state vectors in this space. 
(a) Prove that the complex conjugate of the matrix element of $M$ is given by $\braket{\Phi}{M \Psi}^* = \bra{\Psi} M^\dagger \ket{\Phi}$, where $M^\dagger$ is the Hermitian adjoint of $M$.
(b) Using the result from part (a), or otherwise, demonstrate that if $\ket{\Psi}$ is an eigenstate of $M$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$ (i.e., $M \ket{\Psi} = \lambda \ket{\Psi}$), then the corresponding bra vector $\bra{\Psi}$ is a left eigenstate of $M^\dagger$ with eigenvalue $\lambda^*$ (i.e., $\bra{\Psi} M^\dagger = \lambda^* \bra{\Psi}$).

## Prerequisites
- **Conjugate Symmetry of Inner Products**: The inner product in a complex Hilbert space satisfies $\braket{a}{b}^* = \braket{b}{a}$.
- **Completeness Relation**: A complete set of orthonormal basis states $\ket{i}$ satisfies $\sum_i \ket{i}\bra{i} = 1$.
- **Definition of Matrix Elements**: The matrix element of an operator $M$ between basis states is $M_{ij} = \bra{i} M \ket{j}$.
- **Definition of Hermitian Adjoint**: The matrix elements of the adjoint operator $M^\dagger$ are related to those of $M$ by $(M^\dagger)_{ij} = M_{ji}^*$.
- **Eigenvalue Equation**: An operator $M$ acting on an eigenstate $\ket{\Psi}$ yields the state scaled by a number $\lambda$: $M\ket{\Psi} = \lambda \ket{\Psi}$.

## Hints
- Hint 1: For part (a), express the state $M\ket{\Psi}$ as a new vector, say $\ket{\chi}$, and use the definition of the complex conjugate of an inner product: $\braket{\Phi}{\chi}^* = \braket{\chi}{\Phi}$.
- Hint 2: Alternatively, insert a completeness relation $\sum_i \ket{i}\bra{i} = 1$ twice into the matrix element $\bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi}$ and use the definition $(M^\dagger)_{ji} = M_{ij}^*$.
- Hint 3: For part (b), consider taking the inner product of the eigenvalue equation with an arbitrary state $\ket{\Phi}$, taking the complex conjugate, and applying the result from part (a).
- Hint 4: Because $\ket{\Phi}$ is arbitrary, you can drop it from the resulting inner product to deduce the operator relation acting on the bra.

## Solution

### Step 1: Prove part (a) using matrix elements in a basis
Let us insert two sets of complete basis states $\sum_i \ket{i}\bra{i} = 1$ and $\sum_j \ket{j}\bra{j} = 1$ into the matrix element $\bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi}$:
$$ \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} = \sum_{i,j} \braket{\Phi}{i} \bra{i} M \ket{j} \braket{j}{\Psi} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Take the complex conjugate of the matrix element
Now, we take the complex conjugate of the entire expression. Remember that for complex numbers $(abc)^* = a^* b^* c^*$.
$$ \left( \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} \right)^* = \sum_{i,j} \braket{\Phi}{i}^* (\bra{i} M \ket{j})^* \braket{j}{\Psi}^* $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Use properties of inner products and the adjoint
We apply the conjugate symmetry of the inner product, $\braket{a}{b}^* = \braket{b}{a}$, and the definition of the Hermitian adjoint, $(M^\dagger)_{ji} = M_{ij}^* = (\bra{i} M \ket{j})^*$:
$$ \left( \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} \right)^* = \sum_{i,j} \braket{i}{\Phi} \bra{j} M^\dagger \ket{i} \braket{\Psi}{j} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Reorder and collapse the completeness relations
We can rearrange the scalar terms and re-collapse the completeness relations:
$$ \left( \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} \right)^* = \sum_{i,j} \braket{\Psi}{j} \bra{j} M^\dagger \ket{i} \braket{i}{\Phi} = \bra{\Psi} \left( \sum_j \ket{j}\bra{j} \right) M^\dagger \left( \sum_i \ket{i}\bra{i} \right) \ket{\Phi} $$
$$ \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi}^* = \bra{\Psi} M^\dagger \ket{\Phi} $$
This concludes the proof for part (a).
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Prove part (b) by considering an arbitrary state
Given the eigenvalue equation $M \ket{\Psi} = \lambda \ket{\Psi}$, let us take the inner product with an arbitrary state $\ket{\Phi}$:
$$ \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} = \bra{\Phi} (\lambda \ket{\Psi}) = \lambda \braket{\Phi}{\Psi} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 6: Take the complex conjugate of the equation
Taking the complex conjugate of both sides:
$$ \left( \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} \right)^* = \lambda^* \braket{\Phi}{\Psi}^* $$
Using conjugate symmetry for the right side ($\braket{\Phi}{\Psi}^* = \braket{\Psi}{\Phi}$):
$$ \left( \bra{\Phi} M \ket{\Psi} \right)^* = \lambda^* \braket{\Psi}{\Phi} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 7: Apply the result from part (a)
From part (a), the left side is equivalent to $\bra{\Psi} M^\dagger \ket{\Phi}$. Substituting this in gives:
$$ \bra{\Psi} M^\dagger \ket{\Phi} = \lambda^* \braket{\Psi}{\Phi} $$
Since this equation holds for any arbitrary state vector $\ket{\Phi}$, we can drop the $\ket{\Phi}$ to obtain the relation for the bra vector:
$$ \bra{\Psi} M^\dagger = \lambda^* \bra{\Psi} $$
This concludes the proof for part (b).
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

## Discussion
This problem demonstrates fundamental properties of the Dirac notation and adjoint operators. The result from part (a) is the precise statement of what $M^\dagger$ means in the context of inner products: moving an operator from the ket to the bra corresponds to taking its Hermitian adjoint. 

Part (b) illustrates a crucial corollary: if $\ket{\Psi}$ is a right eigenstate of $M$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, then $\bra{\Psi}$ is a left eigenstate of $M^\dagger$ with eigenvalue $\lambda^*$. For a physical observable, the operator must be Hermitian ($M = M^\dagger$). In this case, part (b) shows that $\bra{\Psi} M = \lambda^* \bra{\Psi}$. Taking the inner product with $\ket{\Psi}$ yields $\bra{\Psi}M\ket{\Psi} = \lambda \braket{\Psi}{\Psi} = \lambda^* \braket{\Psi}{\Psi}$, forcing $\lambda = \lambda^*$, proving that Hermitian operators have strictly real eigenvalues, a cornerstone of quantum mechanics.
