# Problem 4.11

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 11

## Problem Statement

11. A particle of mass $m$ is in a potential $V(x) = v\delta(x - a) + v\delta(x + a)$ where $v < 0$.
(a) What is the wave function for a bound state with even parity?
(b) Find the expression that determines the bound state energies for even parity states, and determine graphically how many even parity bound states there are.
(c) Solve for the even parity bound state energy analytically in the case that $m|v|a/\hbar^2 \ll 1$.
(d) Repeat parts (a) and (b) for odd parity. For what values of $|v|$ are there bound states?
(e) Find the even and odd parity state binding energies for $a \gg \hbar^2/m|v|$. Explain physically why these energies move closer and closer together as $a \to \infty$.

---

## Restatement

A particle of mass $m$ moves in a one-dimensional double delta-function potential:
$$ V(x) = v\delta(x - a) + v\delta(x + a) $$
where $v < 0$. We wish to determine:
(a) The general functional form of the wave function for an even-parity bound state.
(b) The transcendental equation for the even-parity bound state energies, and the number of such bound states determined graphically.
(c) An analytical expression for the even-parity bound state energy in the limit where the dimensionless parameter $m|v|a/\hbar^2 \ll 1$.
(d) The wave function, energy equation, and conditions on $|v|$ for the existence of an odd-parity bound state.
(e) The binding energies for both even and odd parity states in the large separation limit $a \gg \hbar^2/m|v|$, and a physical explanation for their asymptotic degeneracy.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation**: $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\dd^2\psi}{\dd x^2} + V(x)\psi = E\psi$.
- **Delta Function Potential Matching Condition**: Integrating the Schrödinger equation across a point $x_0$ yields a discontinuity in the first derivative: $\Delta \psi'(x_0) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2} \alpha \psi(x_0)$ for a potential $V(x) = \alpha \delta(x-x_0)$.
- **Parity Symmetry**: For symmetric potentials $V(x) = V(-x)$, energy eigenstates can be chosen to be eigenstates of the parity operator, meaning $\psi(x) = \pm \psi(-x)$.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For bound states, the energy $E < 0$. Let $E = -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m}$ with $\kappa > 0$, and solve the free-particle Schrödinger equation in the three regions separated by the delta functions.

> **Hint 2** — Use the parity symmetry to reduce the number of unknown coefficients. Even states have $\psi(x) = \psi(-x)$ (cosine/cosh-like) and odd states have $\psi(x) = -\psi(-x)$ (sine/sinh-like).

> **Hint 3** — Apply the matching conditions at $x=a$: continuity of the wavefunction $\psi(a^+) = \psi(a^-)$, and the jump in the derivative $\psi'(a^+) - \psi'(a^-) = \frac{2mv}{\hbar^2} \psi(a)$. 

> **Hint 4** — For the graphical solutions, define dimensionless variables like $z = \kappa a$ and $g = m|v|a/\hbar^2$, and look for intersections of the straight line $y = z$ with the curves $y = g(1 \pm e^{-2z})$.

## Solution

### Step 1: General Solution and Even Parity Wave Function

For bound states, the energy is $E < 0$. We define $\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar > 0$, so $E = -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m}$.
Away from $x = \pm a$, the potential is zero, and the Schrödinger equation becomes:
$$ \frac{\dd^2\psi}{\dd x^2} = \kappa^2 \psi(x) $$
The general solutions are linear combinations of $e^{\kappa x}$ and $e^{-\kappa x}$. For the wave function to be normalizable, it must decay as $|x| \to \infty$. Thus, the piecewise solution is:
$$ \psi(x) = \begin{cases} A e^{\kappa x} & x < -a \\ B e^{\kappa x} + C e^{-\kappa x} & -a < x < a \\ D e^{-\kappa x} & x > a \end{cases} $$
For a state with even parity, $\psi(x) = \psi(-x)$.
In the central region $|x| < a$, this requires $B = C$, which implies $\psi(x) \propto \cosh(\kappa x)$. In the outer regions, this requires $A = D$.
Thus, the even parity wave function takes the form:
$$ \psi_{\text{even}}(x) = \begin{cases} C' \cosh(\kappa x) & |x| < a \\ A e^{-\kappa |x|} & |x| > a \end{cases} $$
Continuity at $x = a$ demands $\psi(a^-) = \psi(a^+)$, giving $C' \cosh(\kappa a) = A e^{-\kappa a}$.
We can express the wave function entirely in terms of one normalization constant $N$:
$$ \psi_{\text{even}}(x) = N \begin{cases} \cosh(\kappa a) e^{-\kappa(x-a)} & x > a \\ \cosh(\kappa x) & -a < x < a \\ \cosh(\kappa a) e^{\kappa(x+a)} & x < -a \end{cases} $$

### Step 2: Energy Equation for Even Parity

Integrating the Schrödinger equation across $x = a$ yields the discontinuity condition for the derivative:
$$ \psi'(a^+) - \psi'(a^-) = \frac{2mv}{\hbar^2} \psi(a) $$
From our even-parity wave function:
$$ \psi(a) = N \cosh(\kappa a) $$
$$ \psi'(a^+) = -\kappa N \cosh(\kappa a) $$
$$ \psi'(a^-) = \kappa N \sinh(\kappa a) $$
Substituting these into the matching condition and using $v = -|v|$:
$$ -\kappa N \cosh(\kappa a) - \kappa N \sinh(\kappa a) = -\frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} N \cosh(\kappa a) $$
Dividing by $-N \cosh(\kappa a)$ (which is strictly non-zero) and using $\cosh x + \sinh x = e^x$:
$$ \kappa \frac{e^{\kappa a}}{\cosh(\kappa a)} = \frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} $$
Since $\cosh(\kappa a) = \frac{e^{\kappa a} + e^{-\kappa a}}{2}$, this simplifies to:
$$ \kappa = \frac{m|v|}{\hbar^2} \left( 1 + e^{-2\kappa a} \right) $$
To determine the number of bound states graphically, we define the dimensionless parameters $z = \kappa a > 0$ and $g = \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2} > 0$. Multiplying the above equation by $a$:
$$ z = g \left( 1 + e^{-2z} \right) $$
Graphing the left-hand side $y_1(z) = z$ and the right-hand side $y_2(z) = g(1 + e^{-2z})$ for $z > 0$:
- $y_1(z)$ is a straight line through the origin with slope 1.
- $y_2(z)$ starts at $2g$ for $z=0$ and monotonically decreases asymptotically to $g$ as $z \to \infty$.
Since $y_2(0) = 2g > 0 = y_1(0)$ and for large $z$, $y_1(z) > y_2(z)$, there is exactly one intersection for any $g > 0$. Therefore, there is **exactly one** even parity bound state for any $v < 0$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Analytical Solution for Even Parity when $m|v|a/\hbar^2 \ll 1$

In the limit $g = \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2} \ll 1$, the intersection occurs at a small value of $z$.
We can expand the exponential for small $z$:
$$ e^{-2z} \approx 1 - 2z $$
Substituting into the transcendental equation:
$$ z \approx g (1 + 1 - 2z) = 2g - 2gz $$
Solving for $z$ to first order in $g$:
$$ z(1 + 2g) \approx 2g \implies z \approx \frac{2g}{1 + 2g} \approx 2g(1 - 2g) \approx 2g $$
To lowest order, $z \approx 2g$, which means $\kappa a \approx 2 \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2}$, or $\kappa \approx \frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2}$.
The binding energy is:
$$ E = -\frac{\hbar^2 \kappa^2}{2m} \approx -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left( \frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} \right)^2 = -\frac{2m|v|^2}{\hbar^2} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Odd Parity Bound States

For a state with odd parity, $\psi(-x) = -\psi(x)$.
In the region $|x| < a$, the combination must be odd, so $\psi(x) \propto \sinh(\kappa x)$.
For the outer regions, to satisfy the parity condition, we choose:
$$ \psi_{\text{odd}}(x) = N \begin{cases} \sinh(\kappa a) e^{-\kappa(x-a)} & x > a \\ \sinh(\kappa x) & -a < x < a \\ -\sinh(\kappa a) e^{\kappa(x+a)} & x < -a \end{cases} $$
Continuity at $x = a$ is guaranteed by construction.
Applying the derivative discontinuity condition at $x = a$:
$$ \psi(a) = N \sinh(\kappa a) $$
$$ \psi'(a^+) = -\kappa N \sinh(\kappa a) $$
$$ \psi'(a^-) = \kappa N \cosh(\kappa a) $$
Substituting into $\psi'(a^+) - \psi'(a^-) = -\frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} \psi(a)$:
$$ -\kappa N \sinh(\kappa a) - \kappa N \cosh(\kappa a) = -\frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} N \sinh(\kappa a) $$
Dividing by $-N \sinh(\kappa a)$ (assuming $\sinh(\kappa a) \neq 0$):
$$ \kappa \frac{e^{\kappa a}}{\sinh(\kappa a)} = \frac{2m|v|}{\hbar^2} $$
Since $\sinh(\kappa a) = \frac{e^{\kappa a} - e^{-\kappa a}}{2}$, this simplifies to the energy equation:
$$ \kappa = \frac{m|v|}{\hbar^2} \left( 1 - e^{-2\kappa a} \right) $$
In terms of the dimensionless variables $z = \kappa a$ and $g = \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2}$:
$$ z = g \left( 1 - e^{-2z} \right) $$
Graphing $y_1(z) = z$ and $y_3(z) = g(1 - e^{-2z})$ for $z > 0$:
- Both pass through the origin: $y_1(0) = y_3(0) = 0$.
- $y_3(z)$ increases monotonically, asymptotically approaching $g$ as $z \to \infty$.
- $y_3''(z) = -4g e^{-2z} < 0$, so $y_3$ is purely concave down.
For an intersection to exist at some $z > 0$, the slope of $y_3$ at the origin must be strictly greater than the slope of $y_1$.
$$ y_3'(0) = 2g > y_1'(0) = 1 \implies g > \frac{1}{2} $$
Therefore, an odd parity bound state exists if and only if:
$$ \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2} > \frac{1}{2} \implies |v| > \frac{\hbar^2}{2ma} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Large Separation Limit ($a \gg \hbar^2/m|v|$)

When the distance between the wells is large, $g \gg 1$.
The transcendental equations for $z$ are:
$$ z_{\pm} = g \left( 1 \pm e^{-2z_{\pm}} \right) $$
Since $g$ is large, the solution will have $z \approx g$, and $e^{-2z}$ will be exponentially small.
Substituting the zeroth order approximation $z \approx g$ into the exponent gives the first-order correction:
$$ z_{\pm} \approx g \left( 1 \pm e^{-2g} \right) $$
The binding energies are $E_{\pm} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2} z_{\pm}^2$:
$$ z_{\pm}^2 \approx g^2 \left( 1 \pm 2e^{-2g} \right) $$
Substituting $g = \frac{m|v|a}{\hbar^2}$:
$$ E_{\text{even}} \approx -\frac{m|v|^2}{2\hbar^2} \left( 1 + 2 e^{-2m|v|a/\hbar^2} \right) $$
$$ E_{\text{odd}} \approx -\frac{m|v|^2}{2\hbar^2} \left( 1 - 2 e^{-2m|v|a/\hbar^2} \right) $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{E_{\text{even/odd}} \approx -\frac{m|v|^2}{2\hbar^2} \left( 1 \pm 2 e^{-2m|v|a/\hbar^2} \right)}
$$

## Discussion

The exact limits obtained have transparent physical interpretations:
1. **$a \to 0$ (Small separation)**: The two wells merge into a single well of strength $2v$. Our analytical result for the even parity state $E \approx -2m|v|^2/\hbar^2$ precisely matches the exact ground state energy of a single delta function potential $V(x) = -2|v|\delta(x)$. In this limit, the odd parity state ceases to exist, matching the requirement that $|v| > \hbar^2/2ma$.
2. **$a \to \infty$ (Large separation)**: As $a \to \infty$, the two delta-function wells become essentially isolated. A particle bound to a single well $-|v|\delta(x)$ has energy $E_0 = -m|v|^2/2\hbar^2$. The basis states centered on the left and right wells are degenerate. When the wells are brought to a finite but large distance, the finite potential barrier allows quantum tunneling. This level repulsion breaks the degeneracy, splitting the energies into a symmetric (even, lower energy) and antisymmetric (odd, higher energy) combination. The energy splitting $\Delta E \propto e^{-2\kappa a}$ stems from the exponential amplitude to tunnel through the classically forbidden region, and it predictably vanishes as $a \to \infty$.
