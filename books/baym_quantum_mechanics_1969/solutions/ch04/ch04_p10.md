# Problem 4.10

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 10

## Problem Statement

10. (a) The (negative) delta function potential in one dimension can be thought of as the limit of a square well potential as it becomes deep and narrow. Show that the bound state energy and wave function of the delta function are the limiting values of the square well bound states and wave functions.
(b) Show that $S(E)$ for a delta function potential is the limiting value of $S(E)$ for a square well potential.

---

## Restatement

Consider a one-dimensional finite square well potential $V(x) = -V_0$ for $|x| < a/2$ and $V(x) = 0$ for $|x| > a/2$, with $V_0 > 0$. We can approximate an attractive delta function potential $v(x) = v_0 \delta(x)$ (where $v_0 < 0$) by taking the limit of this square well as its width $a \to 0$ and its depth $V_0 \to \infty$ while keeping its integrated "area" constant, meaning $-V_0 a = v_0$.

(a) Show that the single bound state energy and bound state wave function of the finite square well precisely converge to those of the attractive delta function potential in this limit.
(b) Show that the transmission amplitude $S(E)$ for a scattering state in the square well converges to the transmission amplitude of the delta function potential in this limit.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Delta Function Potential Bound State**: The single bound state energy and normalized wave function for $v(x) = v_0 \delta(x)$ ($v_0 < 0$) are $E = -mv_0^2/2\hbar^2$ and $\psi(x) = \frac{\sqrt{m|v_0|}}{\hbar} e^{-m|v_0| |x|/\hbar^2}$.
- **Finite Square Well Bound States**: The equations determining the bound state energies $E < 0$ for even and odd parity states are $p \tan(pa/2\hbar) = \hbar\kappa$ and $p \cot(pa/2\hbar) = -\hbar\kappa$ respectively, where $p = \sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}$ and $\hbar\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}$.
- **Square Well Transmission Amplitude**: The transmission amplitude $S(E)$ for a particle of energy $E>0$ scattering off a finite square well is $S(E) = \left[ \cos \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} - \frac{i}{2} \left( \frac{p}{\bar{p}} + \frac{\bar{p}}{p} \right) \sin \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} \right]^{-1}$, where $p = \sqrt{2mE}$ and $\bar{p} = \sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}$.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — To rigorously take the deep and narrow limit, set $V_0 a = \lambda$ with $\lambda = -v_0 > 0$. Then perform Taylor expansions around $a \to 0$, recognizing that $p \approx \bar{p} \propto 1/\sqrt{a}$.

> **Hint 2** — For the bound states, evaluate the odd parity and even parity quantization equations separately. Show that the odd parity condition yields no solutions, while the even parity condition produces exactly one bound state.

> **Hint 3** — For the bound state wave function limit, match the internal cosine solution with the external exponential tails at $x = \pm a/2$ for $a \to 0$, and strictly enforce the normalization condition $\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\psi|^2 \, \dd x = 1$.

> **Hint 4** — For the transmission amplitude $S(E)$, simplify the dominant diverging term inside the brackets. Use the small-angle approximations $\cos(\bar{p}a/\hbar) \approx 1$ and $\sin(\bar{p}a/\hbar) \approx \bar{p}a/\hbar$, noting that $\bar{p}^2 a \approx 2mV_0 a = -2mv_0$.

## Solution

### Setup

We define a finite square well potential as $V(x) = -V_0$ for $-a/2 < x < a/2$ and $0$ otherwise. To limit to a delta function $v(x) = v_0 \delta(x)$ with $v_0 < 0$, we require that the integral of the potential well matches the strength of the delta function:
$$ -V_0 a = v_0 \quad \implies \quad V_0 = \frac{\lambda}{a} $$
where we define $\lambda \equiv -v_0 > 0$. We then take the limit $a \to 0$ and $V_0 \to \infty$ while keeping $\lambda$ constant. 

### Step 1: Bound State Energy Limit

The bound states of the square well are separated into even and odd parity states. Let $E < 0$ be the bound state energy. The decay parameter outside the well is $\hbar\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}$, and the momentum parameter inside the well is $p = \sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}$.

For the **odd parity** solutions, the condition is:
$$ p \cot \frac{pa}{2\hbar} = -\hbar\kappa $$
As $a \to 0$, $V_0 = \lambda/a \to \infty$, making $p \approx \sqrt{2mV_0} = \sqrt{2m\lambda/a}$. The argument of the cotangent is $pa/2\hbar \approx \sqrt{2m\lambda a}/2\hbar \to 0$.
Expanding $\cot z \approx 1/z$ for small $z$, the left side becomes:
$$ p \frac{2\hbar}{pa} = \frac{2\hbar}{a} \to +\infty $$
However, the right side is $-\hbar\kappa < 0$. Thus, there are no odd parity bound states in the $a \to 0$ limit.

For the **even parity** solutions, the condition is:
$$ p \tan \frac{pa}{2\hbar} = \hbar\kappa $$
Expanding $\tan z \approx z$ for small $z$, the left side becomes:
$$ p \left( \frac{pa}{2\hbar} \right) = \frac{p^2 a}{2\hbar} = \frac{2m(E + V_0)a}{2\hbar} = \frac{m}{\hbar} (E a + V_0 a) $$
As $a \to 0$, the energy $E$ remains finite so $E a \to 0$. Meanwhile, $V_0 a = \lambda = -v_0$. The left side thus evaluates to:
$$ \frac{m \lambda}{\hbar} = -\frac{m v_0}{\hbar} $$
Equating this to the right side $\hbar\kappa = \sqrt{-2mE}$ yields:
$$ -\frac{m v_0}{\hbar} = \sqrt{-2mE} $$
Squaring both sides isolates $E$:
$$ E = -\frac{m v_0^2}{2\hbar^2} $$
This exactly matches the energy of the single bound state for the attractive delta function potential.

### Step 2: Bound State Wave Function Limit

The even parity wave function for the finite square well is:
$$ \psi(x) = \begin{cases} A \cos \frac{px}{\hbar}, & |x| \le \frac{a}{2} \\ C e^{-\kappa |x|}, & |x| > \frac{a}{2} \end{cases} $$
Continuity at $x = a/2$ gives $A \cos(pa/2\hbar) = C e^{-\kappa a/2}$.
Since $pa/2\hbar \to 0$ and $a \to 0$, we have $\cos(pa/2\hbar) \to 1$ and $e^{-\kappa a/2} \to 1$. Thus, $A = C$ in the limiting case.

To find the normalization constant, we evaluate:
$$ \int_{-\infty}^\infty |\psi(x)|^2 \, \dd x = \int_{-a/2}^{a/2} |A|^2 \cos^2 \left(\frac{px}{\hbar}\right) \dd x + 2 \int_{a/2}^\infty |C|^2 e^{-2\kappa x} \, \dd x = 1 $$
As $a \to 0$, the first integral vanishes because the integrand is strictly bounded $\le |A|^2$ while the integration interval $a \to 0$. We are left with the exterior region:
$$ 2 |C|^2 \int_{0}^\infty e^{-2\kappa x} \, \dd x = 2 |C|^2 \left( \frac{1}{2\kappa} \right) = \frac{|C|^2}{\kappa} = 1 \implies C = \sqrt{\kappa} $$
Thus the normalized wave function in the limit is $\psi(x) = \sqrt{\kappa} e^{-\kappa |x|}$. Since $\kappa = -mv_0/\hbar^2$, this translates to:
$$ \psi(x) = \frac{\sqrt{m|v_0|}}{\hbar} e^{-m|v_0| |x|/\hbar^2} $$
This perfectly maps to the bound state wave function of the pure delta function potential.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Transmission Amplitude $S(E)$ Limit

The transmission amplitude for the square well is:
$$ S(E) = \left[ \cos \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} - \frac{i}{2} \left( \frac{p}{\bar{p}} + \frac{\bar{p}}{p} \right) \sin \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} \right]^{-1} $$
where $p = \sqrt{2mE}$ and $\bar{p} = \sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}$.
As $a \to 0$, $V_0 = \lambda/a \to \infty$. Thus $\bar{p} \approx \sqrt{2m\lambda/a} \to \infty$, and the argument $\bar{p}a/\hbar \approx \sqrt{2m\lambda a}/\hbar \to 0$.

We expand the trigonometric functions for small arguments:
$$ \cos \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} \approx 1, \quad \sin \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} \approx \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} $$
Now consider the ratio terms. Because $\bar{p} \gg p$, we have $\frac{p}{\bar{p}} + \frac{\bar{p}}{p} \approx \frac{\bar{p}}{p}$. The second term in the bracket becomes:
$$ - \frac{i}{2} \left( \frac{\bar{p}}{p} \right) \left( \frac{\bar{p}a}{\hbar} \right) = - \frac{i}{2} \frac{\bar{p}^2 a}{p\hbar} $$
Using $\bar{p}^2 = 2m(E + V_0)$ and the limit $a \to 0$, we find $\bar{p}^2 a \approx 2mV_0 a = 2m\lambda = -2mv_0$.
$$ - \frac{i}{2} \frac{\bar{p}^2 a}{p\hbar} = - \frac{i}{2} \frac{-2mv_0}{p\hbar} = \frac{i m v_0}{p\hbar} $$

Assembling the pieces, the inverse transmission amplitude approaches:
$$ S(E)^{-1} \approx 1 + \frac{imv_0}{p\hbar} = \frac{ip\hbar - mv_0}{ip\hbar} $$
Inverting this gives the transmission amplitude in the limit:
$$ S(E) = \frac{ip\hbar}{ip\hbar - mv_0} $$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{limit}} &= -\frac{mv_0^2}{2\hbar^2} \\
\psi_{\text{limit}}(x) &= \frac{\sqrt{m|v_0|}}{\hbar} e^{-m|v_0| |x|/\hbar^2} \\
S_{\text{limit}}(E) &= \frac{ip\hbar}{ip\hbar - mv_0}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

## Discussion

The problem demonstrates physically that the singular delta function potential $v(x) = v_0 \delta(x)$ operates strictly as an idealized limit of a narrow, extremely attractive square well. The short-range geometric details of a realistic well (like the finite width $a$) fall away when observing length scales $x \gg a$. At those macroscopic scales, the wave function experiences a point-like discontinuity in its spatial derivative proportional to the potential's integrated area. The exact limiting procedure verifies that calculations treating short-range atomic interactions or impurities as delta functions produce physically sound energy bounds, asymptotic wavefunctions, and scattering matrices consistent with finite potential barriers.
