# Problem 4.3

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 3

## Problem Statement

3. (a) Calculate for both $E < V$ and $E > V$ the transmission and reflection amplitudes for the potential barrier of Fig. 4-3.
(b) Calculate the transit time of a wave packet through the barrier; what is its form at resonance?

---

## Restatement

Consider a one-dimensional rectangular potential barrier defined by $V(x) = V > 0$ for $0 < x < a$ and $V(x) = 0$ otherwise.
(a) For a particle incident from the left, calculate the transmission amplitude $t$ and reflection amplitude $r$ for both cases $E < V$ and $E > V$.
(b) Calculate the transit time $\tau$ of a wave packet through the barrier. Determine the simplified form of this transit time at transmission resonance.

---

## Prerequisites

- **One-Dimensional Barriers**: The wave function in piecewise constant potentials is a combination of complex exponentials $e^{\pm ikx}$ or real exponentials $e^{\pm \kappa x}$.
- **Transmission Amplitude**: Defined via the asymptotic form $\psi(x) = t e^{ik(x-a)}$ for $x > a$ when an incident wave $e^{ikx}$ impinges from $x < 0$.
- **Transit Time**: The transit time of a wave packet is related to the energy derivative of the phase of the transmission amplitude:
$$ \tau = \hbar \frac{\dd\phi}{\dd E} $$

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For part (a), set up the regions $x < 0$, $0 < x < a$, and $x > a$ with appropriate wave vectors $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V-E)}/\hbar$ (or $q = \sqrt{2m(E-V)}/\hbar$). Apply boundary conditions at $x=0$ and $x=a$.

> **Hint 2** — To smoothly transition from $E < V$ to $E > V$, observe that replacing $\kappa$ with $-iq$ directly transforms the sub-barrier amplitudes to the above-barrier amplitudes.

> **Hint 3** — For part (b), write the transmission amplitude as $t = |t|e^{i\phi}$. Differentiate $\phi$ with respect to $E$. At resonance, $\sin(qa) = 0$, simplifying the derivative immensely.

---

## Solution

### Setup
We establish three regions for the potential barrier:
- Region 1 ($x < 0$): $V(x) = 0$, incident wave $e^{ikx}$ and reflected wave $r e^{-ikx}$.
- Region 2 ($0 < x < a$): $V(x) = V$.
- Region 3 ($x > a$): $V(x) = 0$, transmitted wave $t e^{ik(x-a)}$.

### Step 1: Wave functions and boundary conditions for $E < V$
In the three regions, the solutions to the Schrödinger equation are:
$$
\psi(x) = \begin{cases}
e^{ikx} + r e^{-ikx}, & x < 0 \\
A e^{-\kappa x} + B e^{\kappa x}, & 0 < x < a \\
t e^{ik(x-a)}, & x > a
\end{cases}
$$
where $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V-E)}/\hbar$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Applying boundary conditions
At $x=a$, continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ gives:
$$
A e^{-\kappa a} + B e^{\kappa a} = t
$$
$$
-\kappa A e^{-\kappa a} + \kappa B e^{\kappa a} = ikt
$$
Solving for $A$ and $B$:
$$
A = \frac{t}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{ik}{\kappa} \right) e^{\kappa a}, \quad B = \frac{t}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{ik}{\kappa} \right) e^{-\kappa a}
$$
At $x=0$, continuity gives:
$$
1 + r = A + B
$$
$$
ik(1 - r) = \kappa(B - A)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Transmission and Reflection Amplitudes ($E < V$)
Substitute $A$ and $B$ into the $x=0$ equations:
$$
1 + r = t \left[ \cosh \kappa a - i\frac{k}{\kappa} \sinh \kappa a \right]
$$
$$
1 - r = t \left[ \cosh \kappa a + i\frac{\kappa}{k} \sinh \kappa a \right]
$$
Adding the two equations yields $2 = (1+r) + (1-r)$:
$$
2 = t \left[ 2\cosh \kappa a - i \left( \frac{k^2 - \kappa^2}{k\kappa} \right) \sinh \kappa a \right]
$$
$$
t = \frac{2ik\kappa}{2ik\kappa \cosh \kappa a + (k^2 - \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a}
$$
Subtracting the equations yields $2r = (1+r) - (1-r)$:
$$
2r = t \left[ -i \left( \frac{k}{\kappa} + \frac{\kappa}{k} \right) \sinh \kappa a \right] = t \left[ -i \frac{k^2 + \kappa^2}{k\kappa} \sinh \kappa a \right]
$$
$$
r = \frac{(k^2 + \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a}{2ik\kappa \cosh \kappa a + (k^2 - \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Amplitudes for $E > V$
For $E > V$, we analytically continue by replacing $\kappa \to -iq$ where $q = \sqrt{2m(E-V)}/\hbar$. Using $\cosh(-iqa) = \cos qa$ and $\sinh(-iqa) = -i\sin qa$:
$$
t = \frac{2kq}{2kq \cos qa - i(k^2 + q^2) \sin qa}
$$
$$
r = \frac{-i(k^2 - q^2) \sin qa}{2kq \cos qa - i(k^2 + q^2) \sin qa}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Transit Time Setup
The transit time $\tau$ of a wave packet is given by the energy derivative of the transmission phase $\phi(E)$, where $t = |t|e^{i\phi}$:
$$
\tau = \hbar \frac{\dd\phi}{\dd E}
$$
For $E > V$, we identify the phase from $t = ( \cos qa - i \frac{k^2+q^2}{2kq} \sin qa )^{-1}$:
$$
\tan \phi = \frac{k^2+q^2}{2kq} \tan qa \equiv f(E) \tan qa
$$
where $f(E) = \frac{2E-V}{2\sqrt{E(E-V)}}$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 6: Transit Time for $E > V$ and Resonance
Differentiating $\tan \phi = f(E) \tan qa$ with respect to $E$:
$$
\sec^2 \phi \frac{\dd\phi}{\dd E} = f'(E) \tan qa + f(E) a \sec^2 qa \frac{\dd q}{\dd E}
$$
Using $\cos^2 \phi = |t|^2 \cos^2 qa$ and $q' = m/(\hbar^2 q)$, the general transit time is:
$$
\tau = \hbar |t|^2 \left[ f'(E) \sin qa \cos qa + f(E) \frac{ma}{\hbar^2 q} \right]
$$
At resonance, $\sin qa = 0$ (so $|t|^2 = 1$). The transit time simplifies to:
$$
\tau_{\text{res}} = \hbar f(E) \frac{ma}{\hbar^2 q} = \hbar \left( \frac{k^2+q^2}{2kq} \right) \frac{ma}{\hbar^2 q}
$$
Since the incident velocity is $v = \hbar k/m$, this becomes:
$$
\tau_{\text{res}} = \frac{a}{v} \frac{k^2+q^2}{2q^2} = \frac{a}{v} \frac{2E-V}{2(E-V)}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 7: Transit Time for $E < V$
For $E < V$, the phase is given by $\tan \phi = g(E) \tanh \kappa a$ where $g(E) = \frac{k^2-\kappa^2}{2k\kappa} = \frac{2E-V}{2\sqrt{E(V-E)}}$.
Following a similar differentiation with $\kappa' = -m/(\hbar^2 \kappa)$:
$$
\tau = \hbar |t|^2 \left[ g'(E) \sinh \kappa a \cosh \kappa a - g(E) \frac{ma}{\hbar^2 \kappa} \right]
$$
where $g'(E) = \frac{V^2}{4(E(V-E))^{3/2}}$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
t_{E<V} &= \frac{2ik\kappa}{2ik\kappa \cosh \kappa a + (k^2 - \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a} \\
r_{E<V} &= \frac{(k^2 + \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a}{2ik\kappa \cosh \kappa a + (k^2 - \kappa^2) \sinh \kappa a} \\
t_{E>V} &= \frac{2kq}{2kq \cos qa - i(k^2 + q^2) \sin qa} \\
r_{E>V} &= \frac{-i(k^2 - q^2) \sin qa}{2kq \cos qa - i(k^2 + q^2) \sin qa} \\
\tau_{\text{res}} &= \frac{a}{v} \frac{2E-V}{2(E-V)}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

---

## Discussion

The transit time derived here via the stationary phase approximation represents the group delay of the peak of the wave packet. For $E > V$, at resonance where transmission is perfect ($|t|^2=1$), the transit time differs from the free-space value $a/v$ by a factor that depends on the relative energy compared to the barrier. Notice that as $E \to \infty$, $\tau_{\text{res}} \to a/v$. For $E < V$, the general expression for transit time exhibits the Hartman effect: as the barrier width $a \to \infty$, the transit time $\tau$ approaches a constant, seemingly implying an arbitrarily large effective velocity.

## Related Resources

- **Typical method used**: Calculating transmission amplitudes by matching boundary conditions and transit time via the group delay formula $\tau = \hbar \dd\phi/\dd E$.
- **Alternative approaches**: The transfer matrix method gives identical transmission and reflection amplitudes, often preferred for multi-layer barriers.
- **References**:
  - Hartman, T. E. (1962). *Tunneling of a Wave Packet*. Journal of Applied Physics, 33(12), 3427-3433.

## Notes

The formulation assumes the wave packet is sharply peaked in momentum space. Very broad momentum distributions would undergo significant distortion, complicating the simple definition of a definitive "transit time" tracking the packet's peak.
