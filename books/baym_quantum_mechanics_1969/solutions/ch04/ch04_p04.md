# Problem 4.4

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 4

## Problem Statement

4. Verify Eqs. (4-30) and (4-31) using the expression for the current density given in Problem 10, Chapter 3.

---

## Restatement

Consider a particle of mass $m$ and energy $E$ incident on a one-dimensional potential step of height $V$ at $x=0$, where $E > V$. The wave function is given by:
$$
\psi(x) = \begin{cases}
A e^{ipx/\hbar} + B e^{-ipx/\hbar}, & x < 0, \\
C e^{i\bar{p}x/\hbar}, & x > 0,
\end{cases}
$$
where $p = \sqrt{2mE}$ and $\bar{p} = \sqrt{2m(E-V)}$. Using the one-dimensional probability current density
$$
j(x) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} \left( \psi^*(x) \frac{d\psi}{dx} - \psi(x) \frac{d\psi^*}{dx} \right),
$$
verify that the current density in the left region ($x < 0$) is
$$
j_L = \frac{p}{m} (|A|^2 - |B|^2)
$$
and the current density in the right region ($x > 0$) is
$$
j_R = \frac{\bar{p}}{m} |C|^2.
$$

---

## Prerequisites

- **Probability Current Density**: A vector field whose flux through a surface gives the rate of flow of probability. In 1D, it is $j(x) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} \left( \psi^* \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} - \psi \frac{\partial \psi^*}{\partial x} \right)$.
- **Free Particle Wavefunctions**: The momentum eigenstates corresponding to free propagation have the form $e^{\pm ikx}$, representing motion in the positive and negative $x$ directions.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — Evaluate the first derivative $\frac{d\psi}{dx}$ for both $\psi_L(x)$ and $\psi_R(x)$.
> **Hint 2** — Substitute the expressions for $\psi(x)$ and $\frac{d\psi}{dx}$ into the 1D current density equation. For $x < 0$, remember to properly handle the cross terms between the incident and reflected waves.
> **Hint 3** — Notice how the cross terms in $j_L(x)$ perfectly cancel each other because the coefficients of the cross terms will subtract out when evaluating $\psi^* \psi' - \psi \psi'^*$.

---

## Solution

### Setup

The one-dimensional probability current density is given by
$$
j(x) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} \left( \psi^*(x) \frac{d\psi}{dx} - \psi(x) \frac{d\psi^*}{dx} \right).
$$
We will evaluate this expression for the regions $x < 0$ (yielding $j_L$) and $x > 0$ (yielding $j_R$).

### Step 1: Calculate the current density in the left region ($x < 0$)

The wave function for $x < 0$ is
$$
\psi_L(x) = A e^{ipx/\hbar} + B e^{-ipx/\hbar}.
$$
First, we find the complex conjugate and the first derivatives:
$$
\psi_L^*(x) = A^* e^{-ipx/\hbar} + B^* e^{ipx/\hbar},
$$
$$
\frac{d\psi_L}{dx} = \frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( A e^{ipx/\hbar} - B e^{-ipx/\hbar} \right),
$$
$$
\frac{d\psi_L^*}{dx} = -\frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( A^* e^{-ipx/\hbar} - B^* e^{ipx/\hbar} \right).
$$
Now we compute the term $\psi_L^* \frac{d\psi_L}{dx}$:
$$
\psi_L^* \frac{d\psi_L}{dx} = \left( A^* e^{-ipx/\hbar} + B^* e^{ipx/\hbar} \right) \frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( A e^{ipx/\hbar} - B e^{-ipx/\hbar} \right).
$$
Expanding the product yields:
$$
\psi_L^* \frac{d\psi_L}{dx} = \frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( |A|^2 - A^* B e^{-2ipx/\hbar} + B^* A e^{2ipx/\hbar} - |B|^2 \right).
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Next, we compute the term $\psi_L \frac{d\psi_L^*}{dx}$:
$$
\psi_L \frac{d\psi_L^*}{dx} = \left( A e^{ipx/\hbar} + B e^{-ipx/\hbar} \right) \left[ -\frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( A^* e^{-ipx/\hbar} - B^* e^{ipx/\hbar} \right) \right].
$$
Expanding this product gives:
$$
\psi_L \frac{d\psi_L^*}{dx} = -\frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( |A|^2 - A B^* e^{2ipx/\hbar} + B A^* e^{-2ipx/\hbar} - |B|^2 \right).
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Subtracting the second term from the first:
$$
\psi_L^* \frac{d\psi_L}{dx} - \psi_L \frac{d\psi_L^*}{dx} = \frac{ip}{\hbar} \left( 2|A|^2 - 2|B|^2 \right) = \frac{2ip}{\hbar} (|A|^2 - |B|^2).
$$
Notice that the cross terms perfectly cancel out. Plugging this back into the current density formula:
$$
j_L = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} \left( \frac{2ip}{\hbar} (|A|^2 - |B|^2) \right) = \frac{p}{m} (|A|^2 - |B|^2).
$$
This verifies Eq. (4-30).
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Calculate the current density in the right region ($x > 0$)

The wave function for $x > 0$ is
$$
\psi_R(x) = C e^{i\bar{p}x/\hbar}.
$$
We find the complex conjugate and derivatives:
$$
\psi_R^*(x) = C^* e^{-i\bar{p}x/\hbar},
$$
$$
\frac{d\psi_R}{dx} = \frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} C e^{i\bar{p}x/\hbar},
$$
$$
\frac{d\psi_R^*}{dx} = -\frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} C^* e^{-i\bar{p}x/\hbar}.
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Compute the components:
$$
\psi_R^* \frac{d\psi_R}{dx} = \left( C^* e^{-i\bar{p}x/\hbar} \right) \left( \frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} C e^{i\bar{p}x/\hbar} \right) = \frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} |C|^2,
$$
$$
\psi_R \frac{d\psi_R^*}{dx} = \left( C e^{i\bar{p}x/\hbar} \right) \left( -\frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} C^* e^{-i\bar{p}x/\hbar} \right) = -\frac{i\bar{p}}{\hbar} |C|^2.
$$
Subtracting the second term from the first:
$$
\psi_R^* \frac{d\psi_R}{dx} - \psi_R \frac{d\psi_R^*}{dx} = \frac{2i\bar{p}}{\hbar} |C|^2.
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Plugging this into the current density formula:
$$
j_R = \frac{\hbar}{2mi} \left( \frac{2i\bar{p}}{\hbar} |C|^2 \right) = \frac{\bar{p}}{m} |C|^2.
$$
This verifies Eq. (4-31).
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{
j_L = \frac{p}{m} (|A|^2 - |B|^2), \quad j_R = \frac{\bar{p}}{m} |C|^2
}
$$

---

## Discussion

The expression for $j_L$ clearly separates into a forward-flowing current $\frac{p}{m}|A|^2$ corresponding to the incident wave and a backward-flowing current $\frac{p}{m}|B|^2$ corresponding to the reflected wave. The absence of interference terms in the current is an important feature for energy eigenstates with real values of $p$; it indicates that the total current is the simple sum of independent classical-like currents of probability flowing in opposite directions. The current $j_R$ is purely forward-flowing, consistent with the purely transmitted wave boundary condition. Due to probability conservation, the current density must be continuous across the barrier, so $j_L = j_R$.

## Related Resources

- **Typical method used**: Straightforward evaluation of the current density operator $\hat{j}(x) = \operatorname{Re} \left[ \psi^* \frac{\hat{p}}{m} \psi \right]$ on plane wave superpositions.
- **Alternative approaches**: Writing the wave function in terms of amplitude and phase $\psi = \sqrt{\rho} e^{iS/\hbar}$ and using $j = \rho \frac{\nabla S}{m}$ works well for $\psi_R$ but is less elegant for $\psi_L$ because the superposition of plane waves leads to a phase $S(x)$ that is complicated and oscillating.
- **References**: Shankar, R., *Principles of Quantum Mechanics* (Chapter 5: Free Particle and Potential Step).

## Notes

The problem assumes a plane wave solution, which is unnormalizable. In reality, physical states are wave packets. However, for a nearly monochromatic wave packet centered around $p$, the local current density corresponds exactly to these derived expressions.
