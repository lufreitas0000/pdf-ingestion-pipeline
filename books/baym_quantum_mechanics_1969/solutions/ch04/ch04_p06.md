# Problem 4.6

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 6

## Problem Statement

6. Consider a potential step
$$
V_{\text{I}}(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & x < a \\
V(x), & a < x < b \\
\infty, & x > b,
\end{cases}
$$
where $V(x)$ is some function that we don't know and $a > 0$. Suppose however, that we know the reflection amplitude $N(E) = -e^{2i\delta(E)}$ at $x = a$.
(a) Show that $\delta(E)$ must be real.
(b) Consider a particle in the potential well defined by
$$
V_{\text{II}}(x) = \begin{cases}
\infty, & x < 0 \\
V_{\text{I}}(x), & x > 0.
\end{cases}
$$
Show that to first order in $\delta(E)$ (assumed small), the energy levels of this well are given in terms of the energy levels of the square well
$$
V_{\text{III}}(x) = \begin{cases}
\infty, & x < 0 \\
0, & 0 < x < a \\
\infty, & x > a
\end{cases}
$$
by
$$
E = E_0 - \frac{p_0\hbar}{ma} \delta(E_0)
$$
where $E_0$ is the energy level of the square well and $p_0 = \sqrt{2mE_0}$.
(c) Give a qualitative argument (at least) that $\delta(E)$ must be positive.

---

## Restatement

Consider a one-dimensional potential step $V_{\text{I}}(x)$ given by $V_{\text{I}}(x) = 0$ for $x < a$, $V_{\text{I}}(x) = V(x)$ for $a < x < b$, and $V_{\text{I}}(x) = \infty$ for $x > b$. The precise form of $V(x)$ is unknown, but we are given that the reflection amplitude at $x = a$ for a plane wave incident from the left is $N(E) = -e^{2i\delta(E)}$.

(a) Prove that the phase shift $\delta(E)$ is a strictly real-valued function of energy.

(b) A quantum well is formed by placing an infinite barrier at $x=0$, creating a new potential $V_{\text{II}}(x) = \infty$ for $x < 0$ and $V_{\text{II}}(x) = V_{\text{I}}(x)$ for $x > 0$. Show that, to first order in $\delta(E)$ (assumed small), the energy eigenvalues $E$ are related to the eigenvalues $E_0$ of a simple square well $V_{\text{III}}(x)$ (which has infinite walls at $x=0$ and $x=a$, and $V=0$ in between) by the expression:
$$
E = E_0 - \frac{p_0 \hbar}{ma} \delta(E_0)
$$
where $p_0 = \sqrt{2mE_0}$ is the classical momentum of the unperturbed state.

(c) Provide a qualitative argument explaining why the phase shift $\delta(E)$ must be positive.

---

## Prerequisites

- **Reflection Amplitude and Probability Current**: The steady-state probability current $j_x = \frac{\hbar}{m} \operatorname{Im}\left(\psi^* \frac{\dd\psi}{\dd x}\right)$ must be zero in a region where there is no transmitted wave to infinity, which constrains the reflection amplitude to $|N(E)|^2 = 1$.
- **Stationary States in a Constant Potential**: In regions where $V(x)=0$, the energy eigenfunctions can be written as superpositions of right- and left-traveling plane waves $\psi(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx}$, where $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$.
- **Boundary Conditions for Infinite Potentials**: A wave function must continuously vanish at the boundary of an infinite potential barrier, $\psi(x_{\text{boundary}}) = 0$.
- **Taylor Expansion for Small Perturbations**: The energy shift can be derived by expanding the momentum $k(E)$ around the unperturbed square well values $k_0$.

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — For part (a), consider the probability current in the region $x < a$. Because the potential is infinite for $x > b$, what must the net probability current be? How does this restrict the reflection amplitude $N(E)$?

> **Hint 2** — For part (b), write down the general form of the wavefunction in the region $0 < x < a$ using the given reflection amplitude $N(E)$ at $x=a$. Then apply the infinite wall boundary condition at $x=0$.

> **Hint 3** — The unperturbed square well $V_{\text{III}}(x)$ has energy levels defined by the condition $k_0 a = n\pi$. Relate the perturbed condition for $k$ to $k_0 a$ and expand the energy $E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}$ around $k_0$ to first order in $\delta(E)$.

> **Hint 4** — For part (c), compare the "size" of the potential well $V_{\text{II}}$ to the strict square well $V_{\text{III}}$. Does the true wavefunction penetrate past $x=a$? How does expanding the effective width of a well change its bound state energy eigenvalues?

## Solution

### Step 1: Prove $\delta(E)$ is real

In the region $x < a$, the potential is zero, so the wavefunction can be written as a sum of incident and reflected plane waves. Since the reflection amplitude at $x=a$ is $N(E)$, we can express the wavefunction for $x < a$ natively in terms of the distance from $a$:
$$
\psi_{\text{I}}(x) = A \left( e^{ik(x-a)} + N(E) e^{-ik(x-a)} \right)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

The probability current in this flat region is the sum of the incident and reflected currents:
$$
j_x = \frac{\hbar k}{m} |A|^2 \left(1 - |N(E)|^2\right)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Because the potential is infinite for $x > b$, the particle cannot transmit to $x \to \infty$. Thus, in a stationary state, the net probability current must be identically zero everywhere:
$$
1 - |N(E)|^2 = 0 \implies |N(E)|^2 = 1
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Given the parametrization $N(E) = -e^{2i\delta(E)}$, the modulus is:
$$
|-e^{2i\delta(E)}| = \left|e^{-2 \operatorname{Im}(\delta(E))} e^{2i \operatorname{Re}(\delta(E))}\right| = e^{-2 \operatorname{Im}(\delta(E))} = 1
$$
Taking the logarithm requires $\operatorname{Im}(\delta(E)) = 0$, meaning $\delta(E)$ must be strictly real.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Formulate boundary conditions for $V_{\text{II}}(x)$

For the bounded potential $V_{\text{II}}(x)$, we have an infinite wall at $x=0$, which imposes the Dirichlet boundary condition $\psi(0) = 0$. In the region $0 < x < a$, the wavefunction remains of the form established in Step 1:
$$
\psi_{\text{II}}(x) = A \left( e^{ik(x-a)} - e^{2i\delta(E)} e^{-ik(x-a)} \right)
$$
Evaluating this at $x=0$ yields:
$$
\psi_{\text{II}}(0) = A \left( e^{-ika} - e^{2i\delta(E)} e^{ika} \right) = 0
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Assuming $A \neq 0$, this condition simplifies to:
$$
e^{-ika} = e^{2i\delta(E) + ika} \implies e^{2i(ka + \delta(E))} = 1
$$
Taking the complex logarithm gives the quantization condition for the momentum $k$:
$$
2(ka + \delta(E)) = 2n\pi \implies ka + \delta(E) = n\pi
$$
where $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ is a positive integer labeling the energy levels.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Relate to the unperturbed square well

For the unperturbed square well $V_{\text{III}}(x)$ with strict boundaries at $x=0$ and $x=a$, the reflection amplitude at $x=a$ corresponds to an infinite wall. A wave vanishing at $x=a$ requires $1 + N = 0 \implies N = -1$. Our parametrization $N(E) = -e^{2i\delta(E)}$ then forces $\delta(E) = 0$. The unperturbed momentum $k_0$ therefore satisfies the standard particle-in-a-box condition:
$$
k_0 a = n\pi
$$
Substituting this into our quantization condition for $V_{\text{II}}(x)$, we get:
$$
ka + \delta(E) = k_0 a \implies k = k_0 - \frac{\delta(E)}{a}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: First-order energy shift

The true energy of the particle is $E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m}$. We expand this to first order in the small parameter $\delta(E)$:
$$
E = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left( k_0 - \frac{\delta(E)}{a} \right)^2 \approx \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left( k_0^2 - 2k_0 \frac{\delta(E)}{a} \right)
$$
Recognizing that $E_0 = \frac{\hbar^2 k_0^2}{2m}$ is the unperturbed square well energy, and expressing $k_0$ in terms of the classical unperturbed momentum $p_0 = \hbar k_0 = \sqrt{2mE_0}$, we find:
$$
E \approx E_0 - \frac{\hbar^2 k_0}{ma} \delta(E) = E_0 - \frac{p_0 \hbar}{ma} \delta(E)
$$
To consistent first order, we evaluate $\delta(E)$ at the unperturbed energy $E_0$. This yields the required relation:
$$
E = E_0 - \frac{p_0 \hbar}{ma} \delta(E_0)
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: Qualitative argument for $\delta(E) > 0$

In the unperturbed square well $V_{\text{III}}(x)$, the potential is completely infinite at $x=a$, abruptly forcing the wavefunction to be exactly zero for all $x \ge a$. In the potential $V_{\text{II}}(x)$, however, the infinite barrier is pushed back to $x=b$, and the potential $V(x)$ in the region $a < x < b$ is finite.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

Because $V(x)$ is finite immediately past $x=a$, the wavefunction will leak or penetrate into the classically forbidden (or allowed) region $a < x < b$ before ultimately vanishing at $x=b$. This spatial penetration effectively increases the size of the box confining the particle.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

A fundamental principle of quantum mechanics—readily seen from the uncertainty principle $\Delta p \Delta x \ge \hbar/2$ or the box energy formula $E \propto 1/L^2$—is that expanding the spatial extent of a potential well lowers the kinetic energy of the bound states. Therefore, the true energy $E$ must be strictly less than the tight infinite square well energy $E_0$.
For $E < E_0$ to hold in our derived perturbation formula $E = E_0 - \frac{p_0 \hbar}{ma} \delta(E_0)$, it mathematically follows that the phase shift $\delta(E_0)$ must be positive.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Result

$$
\boxed{E = E_0 - \frac{p_0 \hbar}{ma} \delta(E_0)}
$$

## Discussion

The problem elegantly demonstrates how scattering data (the reflection amplitude and its phase shift $\delta$) encodes boundary information that dictates the bound state spectrum. By measuring the phase shift of a wave scattered off a potential step $V(x)$, we can directly infer the shift in bound state energies when that step is used to form a well. 
The restriction $\delta(E_0) > 0$ reflects the fundamental property that "softening" an infinite potential wall allows wave function leakage, effectively enlarging the confinement region and thus red-shifting the energy levels.
