# Problem 4.8

**Source**: Chapter 4, Problem 8

## Problem Statement

8. Calculate the transmission amplitude $S(E)$ for the potential
$$
V(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & 0 \le |x| < \dfrac{b}{2} \\
V_0 (> 0), & \dfrac{b}{2} \le |x| < \dfrac{a}{2} \\
0, & |x| \ge \dfrac{a}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
Calculate the resonances in $S(E)$ when the shoulders are very high.

---

## Restatement

Consider a one-dimensional double-barrier potential: a central well of width $b$ ($V=0$) flanked by two barriers of height $V_0$ and width $w = (a-b)/2$. Outside the barriers ($|x| \ge a/2$), the potential is zero. A particle of mass $m$ and energy $E > 0$ (with $E < V_0$) is incident from the left. 

1. Calculate the total transmission amplitude $S(E)$, defined such that an incident wave $e^{ikx}$ from $x \to -\infty$ results in a transmitted wave $S(E) e^{ikx}$ as $x \to \infty$.
2. Find the resonant energies $E$ at which the transmission is maximized in the limit of highly opaque barriers ($V_0 \gg E$).

---

## Prerequisites

- **Transfer Matrix**: Connects the coefficients of left- and right-going waves across a barrier.
- **Single Barrier Transmission**: The transmission $t_0$ and reflection $r_0$ amplitudes for a single rectangular barrier of width $w$.
- **Transmission Resonances**: Peaks in the transmissivity $|S(E)|^2$ occurring when the wave trapped between two barriers interferes constructively.

---

## Hints

> **Hint 1** — The problem is greatly simplified by writing the total transmission $S(E)$ in terms of the transmission $t_0$ and reflection $r_0$ amplitudes of a single barrier of width $w = (a-b)/2$.

> **Hint 2** — Match boundary conditions at $x = b/2$ and $x = a/2$ to relate the amplitudes inside the central well to the transmitted amplitude. Repeat for $x = -a/2$ and $x = -b/2$.

> **Hint 3** — You should obtain a Fabry-Perot-like formula: $S(E) = \frac{t_0^2}{1 - r_0^2 e^{2ikb}}$.

> **Hint 4** — For very high shoulders ($V_0 \gg E$), calculate the limiting phase of $r_0$. The resonances correspond to the condition where the denominator magnitude is minimized.

---

## Solution

### Step 1: Definition of Regions and Wavefunctions
Let $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. We define the wavefunctions in the five regions:
- **Region I ($x < -a/2$)**: $\psi_{\mathrm{I}}(x) = e^{ikx} + r e^{-ikx}$
- **Region II ($-a/2 \le x < -b/2$)**: $\psi_{\mathrm{II}}(x) = C e^{\kappa x} + D e^{-\kappa x}$
- **Region III ($-b/2 \le x < b/2$)**: $\psi_{\mathrm{III}}(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx}$
- **Region IV ($b/2 \le x < a/2$)**: $\psi_{\mathrm{IV}}(x) = F e^{\kappa x} + G e^{-\kappa x}$
- **Region V ($x \ge a/2$)**: $\psi_{\mathrm{V}}(x) = t e^{ikx}$

By definition, the transmission amplitude is $S(E) = t$. The width of each barrier is $w = (a-b)/2$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 2: Matching Across the Right Barrier
At $x = a/2$, matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ yields:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F e^{\kappa a/2} + G e^{-\kappa a/2} &= t e^{ika/2} \\
\kappa(F e^{\kappa a/2} - G e^{-\kappa a/2}) &= ik t e^{ika/2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Solving for $F$ and $G$:
$$
F e^{\kappa a/2} = \frac{\kappa + ik}{2\kappa} t e^{ika/2}, \quad G e^{-\kappa a/2} = \frac{\kappa - ik}{2\kappa} t e^{ika/2}
$$
At $x = b/2$, matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ yields:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A e^{ikb/2} + B e^{-ikb/2} &= F e^{\kappa b/2} + G e^{-\kappa b/2} \\
ik(A e^{ikb/2} - B e^{-ikb/2}) &= \kappa(F e^{\kappa b/2} - G e^{-\kappa b/2})
\end{aligned}
$$
Solving for $A e^{ikb/2}$ and $B e^{-ikb/2}$ and substituting $F e^{\kappa b/2} = F e^{\kappa a/2} e^{-\kappa w}$ and $G e^{-\kappa b/2} = G e^{-\kappa a/2} e^{\kappa w}$:
$$
A e^{ikb/2} = \frac{1}{2ik} \left[ (\kappa + ik) F e^{\kappa a/2} e^{-\kappa w} - (\kappa - ik) G e^{-\kappa a/2} e^{\kappa w} \right]
$$
$$
A e^{ikb/2} = \frac{1}{4ik\kappa} t e^{ika/2} \left[ (\kappa + ik)^2 e^{-\kappa w} - (\kappa - ik)^2 e^{\kappa w} \right] \equiv M_{11} t e^{ika/2}
$$
where $M_{11}$ is the $(1,1)$ transfer matrix element for a single barrier of width $w$. Similarly for $B$:
$$
B e^{-ikb/2} = \frac{1}{4ik\kappa} t e^{ika/2} \left[ -(\kappa^2 + k^2) e^{-\kappa w} + (\kappa^2 + k^2) e^{\kappa w} \right] \equiv M_{21} t e^{ika/2}
$$
Thus, $A = M_{11} t e^{ikw}$ and $B = M_{21} t e^{ik(a+b)/2}$.
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 3: Matching Across the Left Barrier
By symmetry, the left barrier from $-a/2$ to $-b/2$ produces an identical set of transfer relations when going backwards. Matching at $x = -b/2$ and $x = -a/2$ yields the incident amplitude (which is 1) in terms of $A$ and $B$:
$$
e^{-ika/2} = A e^{-ikb/2} M_{11} - B e^{ikb/2} M_{21}
$$
(The minus sign on $M_{21}$ arises because $M_{12} = -M_{21}$ for the reflection off the barrier). Substituting $A$ and $B$:
$$
e^{-ika/2} = (M_{11} t e^{ikw}) e^{-ikb/2} M_{11} - (M_{21} t e^{ik(a+b)/2}) e^{ikb/2} M_{21}
$$
$$
1 = t \left[ M_{11}^2 e^{ik(a-b)} - M_{21}^2 e^{ik(a+b)} \right]
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 4: Single Barrier Parameters
For a single barrier of width $w$, the transmission amplitude $t_0$ and reflection amplitude $r_0$ are defined by $t_0 = \frac{e^{-ikw}}{M_{11}}$ and $r_0 = \frac{M_{21}}{M_{11}}$. We can rewrite the bracket in terms of these:
$$
M_{11}^2 e^{ik(a-b)} = M_{11}^2 e^{2ikw} = \frac{1}{t_0^2}
$$
$$
M_{21}^2 e^{ik(a+b)} = r_0^2 M_{11}^2 e^{ik(a+b)} = r_0^2 \frac{e^{-2ikw}}{t_0^2} e^{ik(a+b)} = \frac{r_0^2}{t_0^2} e^{2ikb}
$$
Substituting these back, we find the total transmission amplitude $S(E) = t$:
$$
S(E) = \frac{t_0^2}{1 - r_0^2 e^{2ikb}}
$$
<!-- CONFIDENT -->

### Step 5: High Shoulder Resonances
To find the resonances, we maximize $|S(E)|^2$. This occurs when the denominator is minimized, i.e., $1 - r_0^2 e^{2ikb} \approx 0$.
The reflection amplitude $r_0$ for a single barrier is:
$$
r_0 = \frac{M_{21}}{M_{11}} = \frac{-i(\kappa^2+k^2)\sinh(\kappa w)}{2k\kappa\cosh(\kappa w) + i(\kappa^2-k^2)\sinh(\kappa w)}
$$
For very high shoulders ($V_0 \gg E$), we have $\kappa \gg k$ and $\kappa w \gg 1$. The hyperbolic functions become exponentials, and $r_0$ simplifies to:
$$
r_0 \approx \frac{-i\kappa^2}{2k\kappa + i\kappa^2} = \frac{-i\kappa}{2k + i\kappa} \approx -1 - i\frac{2k}{\kappa} \approx -e^{i 2k/\kappa} = e^{i(\pi + 2k/\kappa)}
$$
The resonance condition $r_0^2 e^{2ikb} = 1$ becomes:
$$
e^{2i(\pi + 2k/\kappa)} e^{2ikb} = 1 \implies 2kb + \frac{4k}{\kappa} = 2n\pi
$$
$$
k \left( b + \frac{2}{\kappa} \right) = n\pi
$$
where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$.

### Result

$$
\boxed{S(E) = \frac{t_0^2}{1 - r_0^2 e^{2ikb}}}
$$
The resonances occur at wavevectors $k_n$ satisfying:
$$
\boxed{k_n = \frac{n\pi}{b + 2/\kappa}}
$$

---

## Discussion

The exact expression $S(E)$ captures the multiple scattering of the wavefunction trapped between the two barriers. It is analogous to the transmission of a Fabry-Perot interferometer in optics. At resonance, when $r_0^2 e^{2ikb} \approx |r_0|^2$, the transmission coefficient $|S(E)|^2 = \frac{|t_0|^4}{(1-|r_0|^2)^2} = 1$, indicating 100% transmission (perfect tunneling) despite the presence of two opaque barriers.

The resonance condition $k(b + 2/\kappa) = n\pi$ shows that the resonant energies correspond precisely to the bound states of a finite square well. The term $2/\kappa$ acts as a small correction to the physical width $b$, representing the penetration depth of the wavefunction into the classically forbidden barrier regions ($1/\kappa$ on each side).
